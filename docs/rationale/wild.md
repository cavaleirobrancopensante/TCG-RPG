## The Old Ways (G)

The Old Ways win by growing more than the opponent can cut down: bigger bodies for the mana, packs of wolves and saplings, and a little extra mana to reach the large creatures early. The faith has no fliers, no card draw and no life gain, so it handles threats by fighting them with its strongest beast, pulling fliers out of the sky and tearing relics apart. Late in the game the Rooted cards (six or more lands) reward a player who has let the land grow deep.

**Briar Fox** · Common · Creature (Beast) · G · 2/1 · Trample (excess combat damage goes to the player).
The faith's one-drop attacker. A 2/1 for one is the baseline rate, and trample on a body this small rarely matters, so it is a gentle bonus rather than a real point of power. The fox slipping through the hedge before the hounds arrive is the English countryside in a single picture.

**Hedgerow Druid** · Common · Creature (Human Druid) · 1G · 1/2 · Tap: add G.
The common mana creature. In set 1 she added mana at upkeep; now she taps for it, which is easier to read and means she can be attacked around or killed before she pays off. Two mana for a 1/2 that ramps by one is fair, and it lets a turn two Druid lead to a four-drop on turn three. A druidess coaxing growth from the soil is a traditional role, shown warmly.

**Thornback Boar** · Common · Creature (Beast) · 1G · 2/2 · Whenever this attacks, it gets +1/+0 until end of turn.
A two-drop that hits like a 3/2 but blocks like a 2/2. It keeps the old set 1 boar's aggressive feel without simply being above rate on both sides. A wild boar rooting up saplings is a familiar menace of the old forests.

**Bramble Spider** · Common · Creature (Spider) · 1G · 1/3 · Reach (can block creatures with flying).
An early answer to the two-mana fliers of the Church and the Covenant. One power keeps it purely defensive, and three toughness lets it block a 2/2 flier all day. Webs strung in the blackthorn hedge are a small, homely image for a faith that guards its sky with its branches.

**Acorn Gatherer** · Common · Creature (Human Druid) · 1G · 1/1 · When this enters, create a 1/1 Sapling token.
Two bodies for two mana, the cheapest way into the token side of the faith. Each body is small, but together they feed pumps and the Stampede. The flavour treats planting oaks as a gift to the far future, which is the patient way this faith thinks.

**Antlered Barrow-Stag** · Common · Creature (Beast) · 2G · 3/3 · Trample (excess combat damage goes to the player).
The spec allows the Old Ways a 3/3 for three with a small bonus, and trample is that bonus. It is the faith's honest three-drop attacker and stops chump blocks from working. Barrow mounds and sleeping kings set it in the old landscape of Britain.

**Oakmoot Sentinel** · Common · Creature (Treefolk) · 2G · 2/4 · Reach (can block creatures with flying).
A sturdy wall against fliers and early ground attackers alike. A 2/4 for three is the faith's defensive rate, and reach is the small bonus. A moot was the old gathering of the land, and a tree that "saw no need to leave" suits it well.

**Sett Badger** · Common · Creature (Beast) · 2G · 2/2 · Regenerate (the first time each turn it would be destroyed, it is tapped and healed instead).
The common that teaches regenerate. A 2/2 for three is a point under rate, which pays for a body that survives its first trade each turn and shrugs off a removal spell. Badgers are famously hard to dig out of their setts, which is exactly what the keyword does.

**Barrowland Bear** · Common · Creature (Beast) · 3G · 4/4 · Rooted: as long as you control six or more lands, this gets +2/+2.
A plain 4/4 for four early, and a 6/6 once the land runs deep. It is the simplest Rooted payoff, placed at common so every Old Ways deck meets the condition. The druids calling the old bear "grandmother" keeps the faith warm and folkloric.

**Huntsman of the Weald** · Common · Creature (Human Hunter) · 4G · 3/3 · When this enters, create a 2/2 Wolf token.
Five power over two bodies for five mana, a solid common two-for-one that is slow enough not to dominate. It gives the faith a mid-curve wolf maker outside the uncommons. The huntsman is a man of the deep woods whose wolves come when he whistles, which is the Old Ways' friendship with the beasts.

**Horned Aurochs** · Common · Creature (Beast) · 4G · 5/4 · Trample (excess combat damage goes to the player).
The common top-end beater. A 5/4 trampler for five gives up a point of toughness for the keyword, so it hits hard but trades with a 4/4. The aurochs was the great wild ox of old Europe, and its size suits a faith of big bodies.

**Old Hollow Oak** · Common · Creature (Treefolk) · 5G · 5/6 · Regenerate (the first time each turn it would be destroyed, it is tapped and healed instead).
A six-drop that is very hard to remove: it blocks, survives and blocks again. A point of power under a 6/6 pays for regenerate. Ancient hollow oaks struck by lightning and still growing are a common sight in English parkland.

**Greening Rite** · Common · Spell (Rite) · G · Add GG.
One-shot acceleration that nets a single mana and costs a card. With set 1's permanent ramp trimmed, this is how the faith makes an early big play without a lasting engine. The flavour, seed into soil and sap into power, is the faith's whole belief in one line.

**Wildsurge** · Common · Spell (Rite) · G · Target creature you control gets +3/+3 until end of turn.
The classic green pump. Since spells are cast at sorcery speed, it is used before combat to push damage through a blocker or win a trade the opponent must respect. One mana for +3/+3 is the old common rate.

**Test of Antlers** · Common · Spell (Rite) · 1G · Your strongest creature fights target enemy creature (each deals damage equal to its power to the other).
The faith's main common removal. It is cheap but depends on having a big creature on the board, and the creature takes damage back, so it never answers a threat for free. Stags settling their quarrels in autumn is the gentlest possible picture of a fight.

**Brought to Earth** · Common · Spell (Rite) · 1G · Destroy target enemy creature with flying.
Conditional removal at two mana, in line with the spec, aimed at the Church's angels and the Covenant's demons. Against a deck with no fliers it is a dead card, which is the price of the low cost. The flavour, every perch is a branch, makes the forest itself the hunter.

**Ivy in the Gears** · Common · Spell (Rite) · 1G · Destroy target enemy relic.
Nature against machines. It answers Technocracy relics and the slow engines of the other faiths, and does nothing else, so two mana is right. Ivy slowly swallowing an abandoned engine is a quiet, unhurried kind of victory.

**Call of the Pack** · Common · Spell (Rite) · 2G · Create two 2/2 Wolf tokens.
Four power over two bodies for three mana, the faith's best common token card. It is good with pumps and with the Antler-King, since the wolves are Beasts. One howl and the hills answering is the Old Ways at their most alive.

**Rowan Charm** · Common · Relic · G · Tap, pay G: target creature you control gets +1/+1 until end of turn.
A cheap repeatable pump that turns every combat slightly in your favour. It costs mana every turn, so it competes with casting creatures, and +1/+1 is modest. A sprig of rowan over the byre door is a real country charm against harm.

**Sower's Basket** · Common · Relic · 1G · Tap, pay 1G: create a 1/1 Sapling token.
A slow token engine for long games, and a use for spare mana once the Rooted lands arrive. Two mana a turn for a 1/1 is a poor rate early, which keeps it fair at common. Sowing by hand in spring and reaping by the cartload is the harvest cycle the faith celebrates.

**Wolfmother of the Fen** · Uncommon · Creature (Wolf Beast) · 2G · 2/2 · When this enters, create a 2/2 Wolf token.
Two 2/2s for three mana, a strong uncommon that is weak to sweepers. It is the clean wolf card of the set and pairs with the Antler-King. The she-wolf who never runs alone is a natural mother of the pack.

**Fowler of the Fens** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Hunter) · 2G · 1/2 · Reach. When this enters, destroy target enemy creature with flying.
A creature that also removes a flier, the faith's uncommon two-for-one against air forces. The small body and the flying restriction keep three mana fair. A fowler was a hunter of wildfowl in the fens, and reading the wind over the reeds is his trade.

**Stonehide Spirit** · Uncommon · Creature (Spirit) · 2GG · 3/3 · Reach, regenerate.
A stubborn defender that blocks fliers and keeps coming back. A 3/3 for four is under rate, paid for by two keywords that make it very hard to get rid of. The spirits patient in the standing stones are a gentle pagan idea, never set against the Church.

**Spearman of the Greenwood** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Hunter) · 3G · 3/3 · When this enters, your strongest creature fights target enemy creature.
Removal on a body, the uncommon form of fight. On an empty board the Spearman fights alone as a 3/3, but next to a large beast he lets that beast do the work. The flavour has him calling the creatures of the wood to settle the matter together, which is how the faith fights.

**Elder of the Oak Circle** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Druid) · 3G · 2/3 · When this enters, create two 1/1 Sapling tokens.
Three bodies for four mana. The power is spread thin, so it shines with team pumps and Wassail. A druidess whose acorns walk beside her by evening is the sort of folk magic that makes the faith charming.

**Green Man of the Hedgerow** · Uncommon · Creature (Spirit) · 3G · 2/4 · Tap: target creature you control gets +2/+2 until end of turn.
A repeatable pump on a body that holds the ground well. It must tap to use its ability, so it gives up attacking to make another creature bigger. The Green Man, the leafy face carved in old buildings all over England, is a perfect gentle emblem of growth.

**Rootbreaker Tusker** · Uncommon · Creature (Beast) · 4G · 4/4 · Trample. When this enters, destroy target enemy relic.
A trampling 4/4 for five that also breaks a relic, so it is never dead against the Technocracy. The body is a point under rate to pay for the removal. Palisades and brass engines as more underbrush says plainly what nature thinks of machines.

**Woodwose of the Fells** · Uncommon · Creature (Woodwose) · 4G · 4/4 · Rooted: as long as you control six or more lands, this gets +2/+2 and has trample.
A 4/4 for five early, a 6/6 trampler late. It is an uncommon step up from the Barrowland Bear, adding trample to the Rooted reward. The woodwose is the wild man of medieval English folklore, and here he comes down only when the land grows wild enough.

**Gift of the Greenwood** · Uncommon · Spell (Rite) · 2G · Target creature you control gets +2/+2 permanently.
A lasting pump, more expensive than Wildsurge because it never goes away. It risks being lost to removal, which is the usual price of putting everything on one creature. The oak that gives slowly and never takes back is the faith's idea of a blessing.

**Trial by Tooth and Claw** · Uncommon · Spell (Rite) · 2G · Target creature you control gets +2/+2 until end of turn. Then your strongest creature fights target enemy creature.
Fight with a pump attached, so the strongest creature usually survives and still kills. It costs a mana more than Test of Antlers for the extra reliability. "The forest holds no courts. It holds contests" sums up how the Old Ways deal with threats.

**Wassail the Orchard** · Uncommon · Spell (Rite) · 2G · Create two 1/1 Sapling tokens. Rooted: if you control six or more lands, create two more.
Two bodies early, four late. The Rooted mode turns a modest card into a strong one for a deck that has let its lands build up. Wassailing the apple trees with cider and song is a real English winter custom, cheerful and harmless.

**Dowser's Hazel Rod** · Uncommon · Relic · 2G · Tap: add G.
A plain mana relic. Three mana for one extra mana a turn is slower than the Druid, but it cannot be killed in combat. The forked hazel stick of the water diviner is a country tradition that suits a faith that finds power in the earth.

**Horn of the Wild Hunt** · Uncommon · Relic · 2G · Tap, pay 2G: create a 2/2 Wolf token.
A wolf every turn for three mana, a strong engine in long games and too slow to matter in a race. It is the uncommon big brother of the Sower's Basket. A horn blown on a winter night that something in the hills always answers is the Wild Hunt as folk tale, eerie but gentle.

**Mistletoe Archdruid** · Rare · Creature (Human Druid) · 2G · 2/3 · At the beginning of your upkeep, add G. Tap, pay G: target creature you control gets +1/+1 permanently.
The set 1 Archdruid made GG every turn and was the clearest balance problem in the faith, so now he adds a single G. The build-around side is his blessing: each turn he can spend that mana to grow a creature permanently. Cutting the sacred mistletoe with a golden sickle is the druidic rite Pliny describes.

**White Hart of the Greenwood** · Rare · Creature (Beast Spirit) · 1GG · 3/3 · Regenerate. Whenever this attacks, target creature you control gets +1/+1 permanently.
A three-drop that attacks freely because regenerate makes it hard to block profitably, and every attack strengthens the herd. Left alone it grows a board quickly, which is the right weight for a rare. The white hart that no king could ever catch is a beloved figure of English legend.

**Antler-King of the Fens** · Rare · Creature (Beast Spirit) · 3GG · 5/5 · Trample. Other Beasts you control get +1/+1.
A 5/5 trampler for five that makes the Beast deck a real archetype. Wolves and Stags from tokens are Beasts too, so the King rewards the faith's token cards. Vigilance from set 1 belongs to the Stoa, so it was replaced with a lord effect limited to Beasts, which keeps team-wide anthems with the Church.

**Yew of a Thousand Winters** · Rare · Creature (Treefolk) · 3GG · 3/6 · Reach, regenerate. Rooted: as long as you control six or more lands, this gets +3/+0.
A wall that almost nothing gets past, which turns into a 6/6 attacker once the land is deep. It gives defensive decks a way to win late. Yews live for thousands of years, and one older than the barrows fits the faith's long view of time.

**Fen-Worm of the Wear** · Rare · Creature (Wyrm) · 5G · 6/6 · Trample, regenerate.
A big, simple threat: a 6/6 trampler for six that shrugs off the first removal spell each turn. It is the faith's answer to decks that rely on one kill spell. The worm that rejoins itself when cut in two comes from the North of England's old worm legends.

**Stampede of the Wild Hunt** · Rare · Spell (Rite) · 2GG · Creatures you control get +2/+2 until end of turn.
A team pump finisher, best after a turn of wolves and saplings. Unlike the Revel's power-only team pumps it adds toughness as well, so it wins combats rather than just racing. The Wild Hunt riding with hooves and horns is the European folk tale at its grandest.

**Hunter's Moon** · Rare · Spell (Rite) · 2GG · Your strongest creature fights target enemy creature. Rooted: if you control six or more lands, your strongest creature then fights another target enemy creature.
Fight early, a double kill late. The second fight is only possible if your strongest creature survives the first or another big creature takes its place. The Hunter's Moon is the October full moon of country calendars.

**Awaken the Grove** · Rare · Spell (Rite) · 3GG · Create three 2/2 Oakling tokens with reach.
Six power over three bodies for five mana, and all of them guard the sky. It refills the board after a sweeper and shuts down flying attacks. The druid singing until the wood remembers it can walk is the faith's folk magic at full strength.

**Circle of Standing Stones** · Rare · Relic · 3G · At the beginning of your upkeep, add GG.
Heavy ramp, moved from uncommon to rare as the set 1 notes asked. Four mana for two extra mana each turn sets up enormous turns, but it adds nothing to the board, so a fast deck can punish it. Stones raised by no hand anyone remembers evoke Stonehenge and the circles of the west.

**Herne, Lord of the Wild Hunt** · Mythic · Creature (Spirit Hunter) · 3GG · 5/5 · Trample. Whenever this attacks, create a 2/2 Wolf token.
A 5/5 trampler that brings a wolf with every attack, so the opponent must deal with him quickly or be buried in hounds. He has no protection and dies to ordinary removal, which keeps him splashy but answerable. Herne the Hunter, the antlered rider of Windsor Forest, is the best-known leader of the Wild Hunt in English folklore.

**Cernoth, the Thousand-Year Oak** · Mythic · Creature (Treefolk Spirit) · 4GGG · 7/7 · Trample, reach. When this enters, create two 2/2 Oakling tokens.
The ramp payoff: eleven power over three bodies. It costs seven and has no protection, which is fair for a mythic the faith is built to reach early. Empires rising and falling in a single ring of its wood captures nature's slow timescale.

**Wheel of the Year** · Mythic · Relic · 3GG · At the beginning of your upkeep, create a 1/1 Sapling token. Rooted: if you control six or more lands, also create a 3/3 Stag token.
A token engine that grows with the land: a sapling every turn, and a stag as well once you are Rooted. It does nothing the turn it lands and can be destroyed by relic removal, so it is strong but not unanswerable. The turning year of seed, flower, harvest and frost is the cycle at the heart of the Old Ways.
