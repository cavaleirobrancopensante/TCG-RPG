# Seed Set: Card-by-Card Design Rationale

## How the set is built

Each faith began with 18 cards: 9 commons, 5 uncommons, 3 rares and 1 mythic. Each faith's mechanics come from what it believes. The **Holy Church** protects and heals, with martyrs, lifelink, angels and exorcism. The **Black Pact** gets power by paying life and draining it from others, because sin always has a cost. The **Old Ways** grow big and many, like nature's abundance: ramp, tokens, trample and reach. The **Revel** is fast and burns bright, but it hurts itself, because excess wounds the reveller. The **Stoa** controls the board, draws cards and waits, which is discipline and reason. The **Technocracy** is efficient and mechanical; in the seed set its cards were colourless bodies that fit any deck, priced slightly under par, and later sets moved most of them onto the C symbol.

Commons are simple and efficient and form the curve. Uncommons add one twist. Rares and mythics are build-around cards. The baseline is roughly *N mana = N/N*, adjusted for keywords and triggers.

The notes were first written for the seed set. Each entry below shows the card as it is today; where a card changed since the seed set, the note says what it was and why it moved. Seed cards that were later cut have no entry here.

## The Holy Church (W)

**Lantern-Bearer Acolyte** · Common · Creature (Human Cleric) · W · 1/2 · Martyr (when this dies, another creature you control gets +1/+1).
In the seed set the acolyte was a one-drop wall, strong defence for a single mana, with 1 power to keep it from being an aggressive threat. Since 1.21 it has Martyr instead of its old blocking bonus: it still trades with early attackers, and when it dies another creature of your choice gets +1/+1, so it sets the Church's tone from the first turn. Acolytes were one of the minor orders, and carrying lit tapers in procession was their duty, so the lantern is a fitting image.

**Novice of the Choir** · Common · Creature (Human Monk) · 1W · 1/3 · When this enters, gain 2 life.
A two-drop that trades a point of power for a sturdy body and 2 life. That is about par: it blunts early aggression and gives life-gain synergies something to count. The choir novice is a young monk learning the sung Office, and his chant is a quiet weapon against evil.

**Cloister Gatewarden** · Common · Creature (Human Monk) · 1W · 2/2 · Martyr (when this dies, another creature you control gets +1/+1).
An on-rate 2/2, so it is a good common. It gives the Church a two-drop that can still attack, which keeps white decks from becoming purely passive. The seed set gave it a blocking bonus; since 1.21 it has Martyr, so its death strengthens the line instead. The flavour is the monastic enclosure, the cloister, guarded by the bell that calls the brothers.

**Martyr of the Pilgrim Road** · Common · Creature (Human Pilgrim) · 1W · 2/1 · When this dies, gain 3 life.
The Church's aggressive two-drop. Trading it away is never a loss, because the 3 life keeps value even if it dies in combat. This is the "martyrdom" mechanic in its simplest form: a death that strengthens those left behind. The pilgrim roads, Santiago above all, were dotted with shrines where travellers who died on the way were honoured.

**Herald of the Morning Bell** · Common · Creature (Angel) · 2W · 2/2 · Flying (can only be blocked by creatures with flying or reach).
A standard common flier, a 2/2 flier for 3. It gives the Church evasion at common so life-gain decks can still close games. Lauds is the dawn hour of the Divine Office, and the flavour ties the angel's arrival to the Church's daily cycle of prayer. As tradition requires, the angel is "he".

**Knight of the Pilgrim Way** · Common · Creature (Human Knight) · 2W · 2/3 · As long as you have more life than you started with, this gets +1/+1.
In the seed set this was a 3/3 vigilance for 3, slightly above par. Vigilance later became a Stoa keyword, so the knight turned into the Church's common lesson in Grace: a 2/3 that becomes a 3/4 while you have more life than you started with. It expresses the knightly orders' vocation to keep the pilgrim roads safe.

**Abbey Man-at-Arms** · Common · Creature (Human Soldier) · 3W · 3/4 · Martyr (when this dies, another creature you control gets +1/+1).
A 3/4 for 4, a solid wall against the Revel and the Old Ways. Its stats are slightly under par on offence, which is the right trade for a defender. The seed set gave him a blocking bonus; he now has Martyr, so when he falls another creature of your choice gets +1/+1. Abbeys kept armed retainers, and he shows force serving charity: he guards orphans, not glory.

**Word of Banishment** · Common · Spell (Exorcism) · 2W · Destroy target tapped enemy creature.
In the seed set the Church's common removal was restricted by power, honest but not premium. Set 2 rebuilt it: it now destroys an enemy creature of any size, but only once it has tapped, usually by attacking, so the Church punishes only those who strike first. Exorcism is a real rite of the Church (in the *Rituale Romanum*), traditionally said in Latin. The faith's removal is framed as driving out evil, not as killing for its own sake.

**Brother of Mercy** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Monk) · 2W · 2/3 · Lifelink. When this enters, gain 2 life.
A good three-drop for life gain: a 2/3 lifelinker plus 2 life up front. It is about a mana's worth above a vanilla 2/3, which fits an uncommon. The seed set showed a nursing sister; the card is now a nursing brother tending the wounded of both sides, in the tradition of the hospitaller orders, which shows the Church's universal charity just as well.

**Inquisitor of the Pale Flame** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Priest Inquisitor) · 3W · 3/3 · When this enters, destroy target tapped enemy creature.
The classic uncommon two-for-one: an on-rate 3/3 that removes a creature when it arrives. The seed version killed anything with power 2 or less; it now destroys a tapped enemy creature, so it answers attackers of any size but can be played around by holding back. The flavour treats the Inquisition as patient investigation of the truth, not caricature, which fits the reverent tone.

**Abbot of the Silent Order** · Rare · Creature (Human Monk Priest) · 2WW · 2/4 · When this enters, gain 3 life. When this dies, other creatures you control get +1/+1 permanently.
A build-around for go-wide Church decks. He stabilises when he arrives, and his death makes the rest of the army stronger for good, so opponents do not want to kill him. That is martyrdom at its strongest, recalling Tertullian's "the blood of the martyrs is the seed of the Church". The silent order evokes Carthusian or Trappist silence. The seed set's abbess became an abbot, and in 1.25 he joined the Priest tribe.

**Seraph of the Dawn Watch** · Rare · Creature (Angel) · 2WW · 3/4 · Flying, sanctified.
The seed Seraph was a 4-mana 3/4 flier with vigilance, a strong, simple rare. Vigilance moved to the Stoa, so sanctified took its place: the Pact can neither target, block nor damage him. The WW in the cost keeps him in heavy-white decks. Seraphim are the highest of the nine angelic choirs, and a seraph keeping watch over the cathedral since before its first stone is a lovely image of guardian angels.

**Reliquary of the First Martyr** · Rare · Relic (Relic) · 3W · At the beginning of your upkeep, if you have more life than you started with, target creature you control gets +1/+1 permanently.
A slow engine. If left alone it turns any creature into a threat, so it rewards board presence. Since set 2 it only works while you have more life than you started with, which ties it to Grace and leaves it idle when you are behind, the right shape for a rare. A reliquary holds a first-class relic, the bodily remains of a saint, and the Church's veneration of relics is the heart of the card.

**Archangel of the Last Vigil** · Mythic · Creature (Angel) · 4WW · 4/5 · Flying, lifelink. When this enters, destroy target tapped enemy creature.
The seed set's finisher was a 5/5 flying lifelinker with removal and 4 life, and these notes warned that it might dominate. It was toned down: he is now a 4/5, the bonus life is gone, and his removal only hits a tapped creature. The Vigil, the night watch before a great feast, and the image of the last candle make him the light that endures. The name naturally recalls St Michael.

## The Black Pact (B)

**Pact Initiate** · Common · Creature (Human Cultist) · B · 1/1 · When this dies, the enemy player loses 1 life and you gain 1 life.
A one-drop chump blocker that drains when it dies. It is weak in stats but always worth something, and it feeds sacrifice and drain decks. The flavour shows the Pact's logic: even a follower's death belongs to his master.

**Blood-Oath Thrall** · Common · Creature (Human Cultist) · 1B · 3/2 · When this enters, you lose 1 life.
A 3/2 for 2 is above par, paid for with 1 life, the Pact's bargain at its simplest. It is a good aggressive common. The fingertip of blood and the debt that "came due later" make clear that the deal is a trap.

**Tomb Crawler** · Common · Creature (Skeleton) · 1B · 1/1 · Deathtouch (any damage this deals to a creature destroys it).
A cheap deterrent: nothing big wants to attack into it. It is fair at 2 mana, since deathtouch makes up for the tiny body. It shows the Pact's habit of destroying what is greater than itself, and since 1.25 it is a Skeleton.

**Grave Chanter** · Common · Creature (Human Cultist) · 2B · 2/3 · When this enters, the enemy player loses 1 life and you gain 1 life.
An on-rate 2/3 for 3 with a small drain, a steady common that slowly tips races. Singing hymns backwards is the traditional inversion of sacred worship that marks the diabolic, making plain what this faith is.

**Ossuary Bone-Walker** · Common · Creature (Skeleton) · 3B · 3/3 · When this dies, create a 1/1 Restless Dead Zombie token.
A 3/3 for 4 is a bit under par, made up for by the token it leaves behind, so it trades for two cards. In the seed set it was the Ossuary Ghoul and left a Restless Bones token; in 1.25 it became a Skeleton and its token a Restless Dead Zombie. It is a fine common for grindy decks and introduces the raising of the dead gently.

**Hellmaw Hound** · Common · Creature (Demon Hound) · 2B · 2/2 · Dread (can only be blocked by Pact creatures and Constructs).
The seed Hound was a vanilla 4/3 for 4, a plain on-rate attacker. Set 2 made it the common dread creature instead: a 2/2 for three that only Pact creatures and Constructs can block, taking the place of the old flying shade once the Pact gave up its fliers. Its demonic flavour stays unpleasant, as it should be.

**Wither the Living** · Common · Spell (Curse) · 1B · Destroy target enemy creature with power 2 or less.
Cheap, efficient removal. It costs less than the Church's Word of Banishment and needs no tapped target, but it only hits creatures with power 2 or less. Destruction is the Pact's specialty, so it deserves the most efficient removal.

**Siphon of Ashes** · Common · Spell (Curse) · 1B · The enemy player loses 2 life and you gain 2 life.
In the seed set this was a 6-point swing for 3 mana; it is now a 4-point swing for 2. It is good reach for drain decks and useless for board control, which is a fair trade. The cracked chalice deliberately profanes a sacred vessel, a sign of the Pact's mockery of holy things.

**Veiled Blade of the Pact** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Assassin) · 2B · 2/1 · Deathtouch, dread.
The seed Blade was a deathtouch 2/2 for 3 that drained 2 when it died. It is now a 2/1 with deathtouch and dread: only Pact creatures and Constructs can block it, and whatever does block it dies, so it hits reliably while staying fragile to removal. Paying assassins in years stolen from their victims is suitably sinister.

**Cinder-Pit Fiend** · Uncommon · Creature (Demon) · 2BB · 4/4 · Dread. At the beginning of your upkeep, you lose 1 life.
A 4/4 for 4 is slightly above par, taxed by a life every upkeep, and since set 2 it has dread as well. It rewards aggressive Pact players and punishes those who keep it too long, which is exactly the lesson: the demon serves but is always hungry.

**Grave Summoner** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Necromancer) · 4B · 2/3 · When this enters, return target creature card with mana value 4 or less from your graveyard to play.
Value reanimation on a weak 2/3 for 5. In the seed set it could return any creature, and these notes worried it would bring back mythics too reliably; it now returns only a creature card with mana value 4 or less, which you choose from your graveyard. Its strength depends on the graveyard: superb late, poor early. "Your rest was never part of the bargain" shows the Pact's contempt for the dead.

**Dread Whisper** · Common · Spell (Curse) · 2B · The enemy discards two cards at random.
Discard as card advantage, a clean two-for-one. In the seed set it cost 2 mana, a little strong since comparable cards cost 3, so it now costs three, at common. Undoing plans with a whisper fits the tempter's work.

**Chalice of Black Vows** · Uncommon · Relic (Relic) · 2B · At the beginning of your upkeep, the enemy player loses 1 life and you gain 1 life.
A drain clock: 2 points of swing per turn for 3 mana, slow but relentless. It was designed as the evil mirror of the Church's seed-set Bell of Vespers, a life-gain relic since cut: one blessed, the other steals. The chalice "never empty" inverts the eucharistic cup.

**Marquis of the Wailing Pit** · Rare · Creature (Demon) · 3BB · 5/5 · Dread. When this enters, you lose 3 life.
The seed Marquis was a 5/5 flier for 4 mana and 4 life, and these notes judged it too efficient when ahead and proposed 3BB. It now costs 3BB and 3 life and trades flying for dread. "Presents his bill at the door" is the whole Pact in one line. Marquis is a rank borrowed from old demonologies.

**Hollow-Crowned Lich** · Rare · Creature (Lich) · 3BB · 3/5 · Deathtouch. At the beginning of your upkeep, the enemy player loses 1 life and you gain 1 life.
A durable defensive engine: a deathtouch wall that drains every turn. It is about par in stats with a strong upside, which suits a rare. The hollow crown is a fitting picture of what sin pays: everything traded, nothing gained.

**Unmaking Rite** · Uncommon · Spell (Curse) · 2B · Destroy target enemy creature. You lose 2 life.
Unconditional removal for 3 mana and 2 life. These notes judged it common-level power at rare, and it moved to uncommon as suggested; since 1.23 it is an instant. "Every unmaking costs the maker a little of his own soul" states the faith's rule directly.

**Belzareth, Prince of Cinders** · Mythic · Creature (Demon Lord) · 4BB · 5/5 · Dread. When this enters, destroy target enemy creature and you lose 3 life.
The faith's bomb. In the seed set he paired unconditional removal with a 5/5 flier and value on death, perhaps the strongest card in the set, and these notes proposed a life cost on entry. He now keeps the removal, loses the death drain, trades flying for dread and costs 3 life when he arrives. The name echoes Beelzebub, and his presence blights the earth, so he is shown as plainly evil.

## The Old Ways (G)

**Briar Fox** · Common · Creature (Beast) · G · 2/1 · Trample (excess combat damage goes to the player).
A 2/1 for 1, the Old Ways' cheap early play, with trample as a gentle bonus. It is on par and forgettable by design. The fox slipping through the hedge is pure English countryside.

**Hedgerow Druid** · Common · Creature (Human Druid) · 1G · 1/2 · Tap: add G.
The common ramp creature. In the seed set it added mana at upkeep; it now taps for G, so a turn-2 Druid still leads to a turn-3 four-drop. That is the faith's core engine and is fair at 2 mana. A country druid coaxing growth from the soil is a traditional figure and a gentle portrayal of paganism.

**Thornback Boar** · Common · Creature (Beast) · 1G · 2/2 · Whenever this attacks, it gets +1/+0 until end of turn.
The seed boar was a vanilla 3/2 for 2, above par, the faith's reward for having no tricks. It now hits like a 3/2 when it attacks but blocks as a 2/2, which keeps the aggressive feel without being above rate on both sides.

**Oakmoot Sentinel** · Common · Creature (Treefolk) · 2G · 2/4 · Reach (can block creatures with flying).
A defensive three-drop that answers fliers, the Old Ways' answer to the Church and Pact air forces. It is about par. A moot is the old assembly of the land, which suits a tree that "saw no need to leave".

**Antlered Barrow-Stag** · Common · Creature (Beast) · 2G · 2/2 · Trample (excess combat damage goes to the player).
The seed stag was an on-rate 3/3 with trample. Testing cut it to 3/2 and a later pass to 2/2, a modest common that still stops chump blocks. Barrow-mounds and sleeping kings call up the prehistoric landscape the Old Ways come from.

**Barrowland Bear** · Common · Creature (Beast) · 3G · 4/4 · Rooted: as long as you control six or more lands, this gets +1/+1.
A 4/4 for 4, an honest common at par, that grows to a 5/5 once you control six lands (Rooted, added in set 2). The druids calling the old bear "grandfather" adds warmth to the pagan faith without making it a rival to the Church.

**Call of the Pack** · Common · Spell (Rite) · 2G · Create two 2/2 Wolf tokens.
Four power across two bodies for 3 mana is efficient, and it builds the "many" side of the faith. It is fair at common, and good with Stampede.

**Wildsurge** · Common · Spell (Rite) · G · Target creature you control gets +3/+3 until end of turn.
The classic green trick, +3/+3 for 1 mana, and an instant since 1.23. It is fair and essential: the Old Ways have little removal, so winning combats is how they handle threats.

**Greening Rite** · Common · Spell (Rite) · G · Add GG.
One-shot acceleration, netting one mana and costing a card. It is fine at common and lets a turn-3 four-drop happen. The flavour (seed into soil, sap into power) states nature's cycle as the faith's theology.

**Wolfsire of the Fen** · Uncommon · Creature (Wolf Beast) · 2G · 2/2 · When this enters, create a 2/2 Wolf token.
Two 2/2s for 3 mana, stronger than Call of the Pack because one of them is a real creature card. Good at uncommon. In the seed set this was the Wolfmother; the name was changed so that every creature in the faith is male, and the old dog wolf who never runs alone is the natural sire of the pack.

**Elder of the Oak Circle** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Druid) · 3G · 2/3 · When this enters, create two 1/1 Sapling tokens.
Three bodies for 4 mana. The power is spread thin, but it is good with team pumps. An old druid planting acorns that walk is the sort of folk magic that makes the faith charming.

**Stonehide Spirit** · Uncommon · Creature (Spirit) · 2GG · 2/3 · Reach, regenerate.
The seed spirit was a big defensive body with reach, above par on toughness. It is now a 2/3 with reach and regenerate: it stops fliers and keeps coming back, paid for by a body well under rate. "Not empty, only patient" suggests the spirits pagan peoples believed lived in the stones.

**Rootbreaker Tusker** · Uncommon · Creature (Beast) · 4G · 4/4 · Trample. When this enters, destroy target enemy relic.
The seed set's Tusked Juggernaut was a plain 5/5 trampler for 5. It became the Rootbreaker Tusker: a 4/4, still trampling, and it destroys an enemy relic when it enters, so it is never dead against the Technocracy. Palisades and siege towers as "underbrush" shows nature's indifference to human works.

**Circle of Standing Stones** · Rare · Relic (Relic) · 3G · At the beginning of your upkeep, add GG.
Heavy ramp: +2 mana per turn from 4 mana. These notes thought that too much at uncommon, and it moved to rare. The stones "aligned to no star anyone can name" evoke Stonehenge-style mystery.

**Mistletoe Archdruid** · Rare · Creature (Human Druid) · 2G · 2/3 · At the beginning of your upkeep, add G. Tap, pay G: target creature you control gets +1/+1 permanently.
The seed Archdruid added 2 mana every turn, the clearest balance concern in the faith. He now adds a single G each upkeep and can spend G to give a creature a permanent +1/+1. Gathering mistletoe with a golden sickle is the druidic rite described by Pliny, a well-chosen historical detail.

**Antler-King of the Fens** · Rare · Creature (Beast Spirit) · 3GG · 5/5 · Trample. Other Beasts you control get +1/+1.
A 5/5 trampler for 5 and a lord for Beasts. The seed version carried vigilance, which went to the Stoa, so the lord effect took its place, limited to Beasts so that team-wide anthems stay with the Church. The twelve-tined crown makes him a sovereign of the marsh, a pagan image of nature's majesty.

**Stampede of the Wild Hunt** · Rare · Spell (Rite) · 2GG · Creatures you control get +2/+2 until end of turn.
A team-pump finisher, best after Call of the Pack or Elder. Fair at 4 mana. The Wild Hunt is the European folk myth of a spectral hunting party, a fitting name.

**Cernoth, the Thousand-Year Oak** · Mythic · Creature (Treefolk Spirit) · 4GGG · 7/7 · Trample, reach. When this enters, create two 2/2 Oakling tokens.
The ramp payoff: 11 power across three bodies. Huge, but it costs 7 and has no protection, which is fair for a mythic the faith is built to reach early. The flavour (empires rose and fell in a single ring) captures nature's slow timescale.

## The Revel (R)

**Tavern Brawler** · Common · Creature (Human Warrior) · R · 2/1 · No abilities.
A 2/1 for 1, the Revel's cheapest attacker. On par. The faith in miniature: enjoyment that ends in violence.

**Vine-Crowned Bacchant** · Common · Creature (Satyr Bacchant) · 1R · 2/1 · Haste (this can attack the turn it comes into play).
A hasty 2/1 for 2, slightly under par in stats to pay for haste. A good common for racing. The Bacchant (from Bacchus) dancing from the wine-press to the battle line is the faith's pagan-classical root, and since 1.25 he is a satyr.

**Masquerade Duelist** · Common · Creature (Human Duelist) · 1R · 2/1 · First strike (this deals combat damage before creatures without first strike).
The seed duelist was a 2/2 that attacked as a 3/2. It is now a 2/1 with first strike, which wins most early trades and makes small blockers think twice. The mask hiding the blade shows the darker side of courtly pleasure.

**Fire-Eater of the Feast** · Common · Creature (Human Performer) · 2R · 2/2 · When this enters, deal 1 damage to any enemy creature or the enemy player.
A 2/2 for 3 with a ping, about par. It kills x/1s, and the faith's flexible burn can go face. The flavour (applause, then silence) is darkly comic, which keeps the Revel "slightly evil" rather than villainous.

**Gluttonous Baron** · Common · Creature (Human Noble) · 2R · 4/3 · When this enters, you lose 2 life.
A 4/3 for 3, a full point above par, paid for in life. The Revel's mirror of the Pact's Thrall, but here the cost is self-indulgence rather than a pact. The gluttony makes "excess hurts the reveller" concrete.

**Carousing Lancer** · Common · Creature (Human Knight) · 3R · 4/3 · Haste (this can attack the turn it comes into play).
A hasty 4/3 for 4, an on-rate surprise attacker and a good common. The knight charging with his goblet unspilt shows the faith's reckless glamour.

**Toast of Fire** · Common · Spell (Revelry) · R · Deal 2 damage to any enemy creature or the enemy player.
The faith's basic burn, 2 damage for 1 mana to a creature or the face. On par and flexible. "To your health!" before lighting the brandy is just the right joke.

**Banquet Flambe** · Common · Spell (Revelry) · 2R · Deal 4 damage to target enemy creature.
The faith's common removal for midsize threats. Creature-only to balance the extra damage. Fair at 3 mana. The accent was later dropped from the name so that it reads cleanly on every screen.

**Duelist of the Velvet Court** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Noble Duelist) · 2R · 2/2 · First strike. Whenever this attacks, it gets +1/+1 until end of turn.
The seed version was a hasty 3/2 that attacked as a 4/3, perhaps the best uncommon attacker in the set, and these notes suggested dropping haste. It did: he is now a 2/2 with first strike who attacks as a 3/3, and since 1.23 a Noble Duelist. "Never lost a duel, a wager, or an argument about wine" is pure Revel.

**Masked Firebreather** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Performer) · 2R · 2/2 · Whenever this attacks, deal 1 damage to any enemy creature or the enemy player.
A repeatable pinger that needs to attack. A fair uncommon: weak stats, strong upside if it survives. Its first attack happens a turn later than the Juggler's, which separates the uncommon from the rare.

**Hierophant of the Bacchanal** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Bacchant) · 3R · 3/3 · Haste. When this enters, creatures you control get +1/+0 until end of turn.
A hasty 3/3 plus a team pump, a turn of all-out attack. The seed version gave +2/+0, too much on a hasty body; it now gives +1/+0. Good at 4 mana. A hierophant is a priest of mysteries, and here he leads an orgy of battle.

**Bottomless Wine Cask** · Uncommon · Relic (Relic) · 2R · At the beginning of your upkeep, rummage (draw a card, then discard a card).
The seed Cask drew a card every turn and cost life, a card-advantage engine strong for an uncommon. It now rummages each upkeep (draw a card, then discard a card of your choice) and costs no life: card quality, never card advantage. Its original flavour, pleasure that never ends as its own slow wound, is the clearest moral statement in the faith.

**Pyre-Dancer's Whirl** · Uncommon · Spell (Revelry) · 2R · Deal 2 damage to each enemy creature. You lose 2 life.
A one-sided sweeper. Very strong against tokens (Old Ways, Technocracy servos), so it is good that it costs life. Dancers spinning closer to the fire are the image of excess.

**Salamander Juggler** · Rare · Creature (Human Performer) · 1R · 2/1 · Haste. Whenever this attacks, deal 1 damage to any enemy creature or the enemy player.
The seed Juggler was a hasty 2/2 that dealt 3 damage on turn 2. It is now a 2/1: still hasty, still pinging every time it attacks, but it dies to any ping. A strong rare at the bottom of the curve. The "salamander" is the traditional elemental of fire, and a juggler handling living flames is a good fantasy image.

**Archduke of the Endless Feast** · Rare · Creature (Human Noble) · 2RR · 5/4 · Haste. At the beginning of your upkeep, you lose 1 life.
A hasty 5/4 for 4 is well above par, taxed by an upkeep drain. Like the Pact's Fiend, but hasty and more dangerous. Strong but self-defeating in long games, so it is well tuned. The eleven-year banquet and the complaining liver are the Revel's gentle self-mockery.

**Grand Conflagration** · Rare · Spell (Revelry) · 2RR · Deal 5 damage to any enemy creature or the enemy player.
Big flexible burn: kills almost anything or finishes a player. Fair at 4 mana and rare. The unplanned encore after the fireworks is the faith's comic ruin.

**Vessar, Master of the Undying Feast** · Mythic · Creature (Human Noble) · 3RR · 0/5 · Idle (at the end of your turn, if this is untapped and didn't attack): deal 1 damage to each enemy creature and 2 damage to the enemy player.
The seed mythic was a hasty 5/5 held in check by an upkeep drain. Set 2 made him the Idle mythic: a 0/5 host who never fights and, each turn he lounges, deals 1 damage to each enemy creature and 2 to the enemy player. He was first drawn as a hostess; he is now a nobleman, the host whose guests "do not rise again", the faith's "slightly evil" side made explicit, and never a warrior.

## The Stoa (U)

**Owl of the Archive** · Common · Creature (Bird) · U · 1/1 · When this enters, foresight 2 (look at the top two cards of your library, put any of them back on top and the rest on the bottom).
The seed set's Owl Familiar was a 1/1 flier for 1. The Stoa gave up its fliers in set 2, so the owl lost its wings and became a quiet watcher that looks at the top two cards of your library when it enters. An owl reading by sitting on books is gentle scholarly humour.

**Lamp-Lit Scribe** · Common · Creature (Human Scholar) · 1U · 1/2 · When this enters, draw a card.
A two-drop that replaces itself, the classic blue value creature. Fair at 2 mana. Copying text changes the copier, which echoes the monastic scriptoria where learning was preserved.

**Bronze-Shield Hoplite** · Common · Creature (Human Hoplite) · 1U · 2/4 · Defender (can't attack).
A cheap wall that stops early aggression. Good for 2 mana, and defender keeps it honest; since 1.23 it is a Hoplite. "What is in my power is to hold this ground" is straight Epictetus: the Stoic distinction between what is in our power and what is not.

**Disciplined Phalanx** · Common · Creature (Human Hoplite) · 2U · 2/4 · Vigilance (doesn't tap to attack).
A defensive three-drop that can still attack safely. On par. "Forty shields, one mind" is the Stoic ideal of the ordered self expressed as formation.

**Tower Stargazer** · Common · Creature (Human Astronomer) · 2U · 2/2 · When this enters, foresight 3 (look at the top three cards of your library, put any of them back on top and the rest on the bottom).
The seed Stargazer was a 2/3 flier plus a card for 4 mana. Without flying it moved down to three mana and swapped its card for a foresight 3. The silver-and-glass balcony fits the faith's colours.

**Wandering Cynic** · Common · Creature (Human Philosopher) · 3U · 3/3 · When this enters, return target enemy creature to its owner's hand.
A tempo creature: an on-rate 3/3 that sends an enemy creature of your choice back to its owner's hand. Good at common, since bounce is temporary. In the seed set it was the Wandering Peripatetic, a learned joke on Aristotle's walking school; it was renamed because the Stoa descends from the Cynics.

**Measured Rebuke** · Common · Spell (Discourse) · U · Return target enemy creature to its owner's hand.
The seed Rebuke cost 2 mana, a little weak, and these notes asked for U. It now costs U, and since 1.23 it is an instant. "Reconsider." is a perfect one-word flavour line.

**Hour of Contemplation** · Common · Spell (Meditation) · 2U · Draw two cards.
Plain card advantage at the standard 3-mana rate. The flavour (stillness, clarity, then the answer) is the Stoic order of thought.

**Binding Argument** · Common · Spell (Discourse) · 1U · Tap target enemy creature. Draw a card.
A cantrip that removes a blocker or stops an attacker for a turn. In the seed set it cost 1 mana, which outclassed the other commons, so it now costs 2. The brute stopping to consider the premise is a good image for reason defeating force.

**Dialectician of the Porch** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Philosopher) · 2U · 2/2 · Whenever this attacks, tap target enemy creature.
An attacker that removes a blocker each attack. On-rate stats with strong pressure. "The Porch" is literally the Stoa, the painted colonnade in Athens where Zeno taught.

**Great-Owl of the Archive** · Uncommon · Creature (Bird) · 3U · 2/3 · Flying. At the beginning of your upkeep, foresight 2.
The seed set's Archive Great-Owl was a plain 3/3 flier for 4, and these notes suggested a small trigger. It now has one: a 2/3 flier that gives foresight 2 every upkeep, one of the Stoa's two small Bird fliers.

**Strategos of the Shieldwall** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Hoplite) · 3U · 3/3 · Vigilance. Other Hoplite creatures you control get +0/+1.
The seed version was a 3/5 vigilance for 4 with a tempo trigger, perhaps the best uncommon in the faith. It is now a 3/3 vigilance lord who gives other Hoplites +0/+1. A strategos is a Greek general, which suits the Hellenic flavour of the faith.

**Orrery of Fixed Stars** · Uncommon · Relic (Relic) · 3U · At the beginning of your upkeep, tap target enemy creature.
A permanent blocker-remover. Because it fires on your upkeep, it stops a blocker every turn but does not stop attacks. Fair at 4 mana. The orrery's "fixed order" evokes the Stoic cosmos governed by reason (logos).

**Stillness of Mind** · Uncommon · Spell (Meditation) · 3U · Tap all enemy creatures. Draw a card.
A finisher disguised as control: clears all blockers for one attack and replaces itself. Strong but fair at 4 mana. An army "sitting down" at a lecture on vanity is a good joke.

**Sage of the Long Patience** · Rare · Creature (Human Philosopher) · 2UU · 2/4 · Vigilance. When this enters, draw two cards.
A sturdy blocker plus two cards for 4 mana is strong value. A good rare and the faith's long-game build-around. "The obstacle is the way" paraphrases Marcus Aurelius (*Meditations* 5.20).

**Codex of the Unshaken Mind** · Rare · Relic (Relic) · 2UU · At the beginning of your upkeep, draw a card.
An extra card per turn with no drawback for 4 mana. Strong in a long game and does nothing the turn it is played, so it fits the faith. Compare the Revel's Cask: discipline gets the cards without the wound, and that is the moral point.

**Aristeon, the Unmoved Sage** · Rare · Creature (Human Philosopher) · 4UU · 4/5 · Vigilance. When this enters, return target enemy creature to its owner's hand and draw two cards.
The seed set's control mythic: a sturdy vigilant body, a tempo swing and two cards. It moved down to rare and lost flying, since bounce is temporary and 4 power is a slow clock. The name means "best", and the "Unmoved" title calls up Stoic *apatheia*, a philosopher so free of passion that he "kept rising".

## The Technocracy (C)

**Brass Scuttler** · Common · Creature (Construct) · 1 · 1/1 · Sacrifice this: put a +1/+1 counter on target Construct you control.
A generic 1/1 for 1, deliberately weak, since cards any deck can play are priced below par. It now melts itself down for a +1/+1 counter on a Construct, which does little outside the Technocracy.

**Clockwork Hound** · Common · Creature (Construct Hound) · 1C · 2/2 · Overclock 2 (you may pay 2 more as you play this). If overclocked, it enters with two +1/+1 counters.
The seed hound was a vanilla 2/2 for 2 that fit any deck. It now costs C and has Overclock: a 2/2 on turn two or a 4/4 on turn four. Its "most honest loyalty" line sums up the faith's cold transactional ethic.

**Rivetwall Sentry** · Common · Creature (Construct) · 1C · 0/3 · Assembly (this gets +1/+1 for each other Construct you control).
The seed sentry was a pure wall any deck could use to stop early aggression. It now costs C, has no defender, and has Assembly, so it grows into an attacker beside other Constructs.

**Tinkerer's Apprentice** · Common · Creature (Human Artificer) · 1C · 1/1 · When this enters, create a 1/1 Servo Construct token.
Two bodies for 2 mana, fair and token-friendly, and now on the C symbol. The "better mouse" flavour shows the faith's incremental progress.

**Piston Laborer** · Common · Creature (Construct) · 2C · 3/3 · (No abilities.)
A 3/3 for 3 requiring a Foundry. The C symbol rewards committed Technocracy decks with full-rate stats. Its tireless work suits the faith's efficiency over passion.

**Foundry Golem** · Common · Creature (Construct) · 4 · 3/4 · Overclock 2 (you may pay 2 more as you play this). If overclocked, it enters with two +1/+1 counters.
A 3/4 for 4, slightly under par as befits a generic card, with Overclock added in set 2 so it can arrive as a 5/6 for six. "Animated by arithmetic" is the faith in two words.

**Calibrated Discharge** · Common · Spell (Device) · 1 · Put a +1/+1 counter on target Construct you control. Overclock 1C (you may pay 1C more as you play this). If overclocked, also deal 2 damage to target enemy creature.
The seed version was weak removal for any deck: 3 mana for 2 damage. It now puts a +1/+1 counter on a Construct for one mana, and its 2 damage needs an Overclock that only a Foundry can pay, so faiths keep their identity.

**Arc-Welder Construct** · Rare · Creature (Construct) · 3C · 3/3 · Overclock 2. If overclocked, when this enters, deal 3 damage to target enemy creature.
The seed version was a 2/3 with 2 flexible damage for 4 in any deck, which gave the Stoa and the Old Ways an answer they should not have. It now costs C, is a 3/3, and its 3 damage needs Overclock and hits only creatures.

**Bronze Phalanx Automaton** · Common · Creature (Construct) · 3C · 3/4 · (No abilities.)
The seed version was a 4/5 vigilance for 5. Vigilance went to the Stoa, and it is now a plain 3/4 for four on the C symbol. The flavour (the Stoa taught discipline, the Technocracy built it) neatly contrasts virtue and mechanism.

**Siege Automaton** · Uncommon · Creature (Construct) · 2C · 5/5 · Upkeep 2 (at the beginning of your upkeep, pay 2 or sacrifice this).
The seed version was a 5/4 trampler for 5. Trample belongs to the Old Ways, so it is now a 5/5 for three that costs two mana every upkeep.

**Galvanic Coil** · Uncommon · Relic (Engine) · 2C · At the beginning of your upkeep, deal 1 damage to target enemy creature.
The seed Coil was repeated damage for any deck that could also hit players, and these notes warned it would be an auto-include. It now costs C and hits only creatures, picking off x/1s each upkeep.

**Self-Mending Forge** · Uncommon · Relic (Workshop) · 3C · At the beginning of your upkeep, create a 1/1 Servo Construct token.
A token engine. Slow, but it overwhelms long games. Fair at 4 mana, and now on the C symbol so other faiths cannot borrow it. The forge "tending itself" suits the faith's cold self-sufficiency.

**Perpetual Engine** · Rare · Relic (Engine) · 2 · Your Technocracy cards cost 2 less to play. This reduces only generic mana. Upkeep 1.
The seed Engine was strong ramp for any deck, the colourless Circle of Standing Stones. It now reduces only Technocracy costs and asks for one mana each upkeep, so it is useless elsewhere.

**Grand Foundry Colossus** · Rare · Creature (Construct) · 3C · 6/6 · Upkeep 3.
The seed Colossus was a plain 6/6 trampler for 6. It is now a 6/6 for four with Upkeep 3, a build-around for decks whose reducers leave mana free to feed it.

**The Prime Engine** · Mythic · Creature (Construct) · 5CC · 4/4 · Assembly. When this enters, create two 1/1 Servo Construct tokens.
The seed mythic put 10 power across four bodies for 7. It is now a 4/4 with Assembly that brings two Servos, so it is at least a 6/6 at once. "Every other machine is its echo" makes it the faith's heart.

## What the seed notes asked for

These were the open questions at the end of the seed set, with what became of each.

- **Mythic power gap.** Belzareth and the Archangel far outclassed the other four mythics. Belzareth now pays 3 life when he enters, and the Archangel's removal hits only tapped creatures.
- **Ramp density.** Archdruid, Circle of Standing Stones, Brass Dynamo and Perpetual Engine together allowed turn-4 six-drops. The Archdruid makes one G, the Circle moved to rare, Brass Dynamo was cut and the Perpetual Engine helps only Technocracy cards.
- **The Old Ways had no removal.** They now fight (Test of Antlers and its kin) and pull fliers down (Brought to Earth).
- **Colourless auto-includes.** Galvanic Coil and Perpetual Engine moved to the C symbol or to Technocracy-only effects.
- **The Stoa's tap effects were offensive.** Freeze and instant bounce now give it real defensive tempo.
- **Bounce was under rate.** Measured Rebuke costs U.
- **Unmaking Rite** moved from rare to uncommon.
- **Rare filler.** Quicksilver Lancer and Skywarden were cut, and the Colossus gained an upkeep that makes it a build-around.
- **Curve.** The seed set had a crowded 4-slot in every faith; later sets filled the curve at both ends.
