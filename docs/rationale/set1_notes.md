# Seed Set: Card-by-Card Design Rationale

## How the set is built

Each faith has 18 cards: 9 commons, 5 uncommons, 3 rares and 1 mythic. Each faith's mechanics come from what it believes. The **Holy Church** protects and heals, with guardians, lifelink, angels and exorcism. The **Black Covenant** gets power by paying life and draining it from others, because sin always has a cost. The **Old Ways** grow big and many, like nature's abundance: ramp, tokens, trample and reach. The **Revel** is fast and burns bright, but it hurts itself, because excess wounds the reveller. The **Stoa** controls the board, draws cards and waits, which is discipline and reason. The **Technocracy** is efficient and mechanical: colourless bodies that fit any deck, priced slightly under par.

Commons are simple and efficient and form the curve. Uncommons add one twist. Rares and mythics are build-around cards. The baseline is roughly *N mana ≈ N/N*, adjusted for keywords and triggers.

## The Holy Church (W)

**Lantern-Bearer Acolyte** · Common · Creature (Human Cleric) · W · 1/2 · Guardian.
A one-drop that is really a 1/4 when blocking. That is strong defence for a single mana, and the 1 power keeps it from being an aggressive threat. It sets the Church's tone from the first turn: the faith holds the line. Acolytes were one of the minor orders, and carrying lit tapers in procession was their duty, so the lantern is a fitting image.

**Novice of the Choir** · Common · Creature (Human Cleric) · 1W · 1/3 · When this enters, gain 2 life.
A two-drop that trades a point of power for a sturdy body and 2 life. That is about par: it blunts early aggression and gives life-gain synergies something to count. The choir novice is a young religious learning the sung Office, and her singing is a quiet weapon against evil.

**Cloister Gatewarden** · Common · Creature (Human Monk) · 1W · 2/2 · Guardian.
An on-rate 2/2 that blocks as a 2/4, so it is a good common. It gives the Church a two-drop that can still attack, which keeps white decks from becoming purely passive. The flavour is the monastic enclosure, the cloister, guarded by the bell that calls the brothers.

**Martyr of the Pilgrim Road** · Common · Creature (Human Pilgrim) · 1W · 2/1 · When this dies, gain 3 life.
The Church's aggressive two-drop. Trading it away is never a loss, because the 3 life keeps value even if it dies in combat. This is the "martyrdom" mechanic in its simplest form: a death that strengthens those left behind. The pilgrim roads, Santiago above all, were dotted with shrines where travellers who died on the way were honoured.

**Herald of the Morning Bell** · Common · Creature (Angel) · 2W · 2/2 · Flying.
A standard common flier, a 2/2 flier for 3. It gives the Church evasion at common so life-gain decks can still close games. Lauds is the dawn hour of the Divine Office, and the flavour ties the angel's arrival to the Church's daily cycle of prayer. As tradition requires, the angel is "he".

**Knight of the Pilgrim Way** · Common · Creature (Human Knight) · 2W · 3/3 · Vigilance.
A 3/3 for 3 with vigilance is slightly above par. That is acceptable at common for the faith that likes to attack and defend at once. It expresses the knightly orders' vocation to keep the pilgrim roads safe. The mention of Lent and Pentecost places it in the liturgical year.

**Abbey Man-at-Arms** · Common · Creature (Human Soldier) · 3W · 3/4 · Guardian.
A 3/4 for 4 that blocks as a 3/6. It is a solid wall against the Revel and the Old Ways. Its stats are slightly under par on offence, which is the right trade for a defender. Abbeys kept armed retainers, and the flavour shows force serving charity: he guards orphans, not glory.

**Rite of Benediction** · Common · Spell (Blessing) · 1W · Target creature you control gets +2/+2 until end of turn. Gain 2 life.
A combat trick that leaves value behind even when the fight goes badly. It is fair at 2 mana, a little better than a plain +2/+2 because of the life. A benediction is a priestly blessing, and the "Go forth" in the flavour echoes the dismissal of the Mass.

**Word of Banishment** · Common · Spell (Exorcism) · 2W · Destroy target enemy creature with power 3 or less.
The Church's common removal. It is restricted by power and costs 3, so it is honest but not premium, and it cannot answer the biggest threats. Exorcism is a real rite of the Church (in the *Rituale Romanum*), traditionally said in Latin, which the flavour's "old tongue" points to. The faith's removal is framed as driving out evil, not as killing for its own sake.

**Sister of Mercy** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Cleric) · 2W · 2/3 · Lifelink. When this enters, gain 2 life.
A good three-drop for life gain: a 2/3 lifelinker plus 2 life up front. It is about a mana's worth above a vanilla 2/3, which fits an uncommon. She is a nursing sister tending the wounded of both sides, in the tradition of the hospitaller orders, a traditional role for women that shows the Church's universal charity.

**Inquisitor of the Pale Flame** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Inquisitor) · 3W · 3/3 · When this enters, destroy target enemy creature with power 2 or less.
A creature that removes a creature: an on-rate 3/3 that kills a small threat when it arrives, the classic uncommon two-for-one. The power-2 limit keeps it from answering midrange bodies. The flavour treats the Inquisition as patient investigation of the truth, not caricature, which fits the reverent tone.

**Paladin of the Seventh Gate** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Paladin) · 3W · 3/3 · Vigilance, lifelink.
Two keywords on an on-rate 3/3 for 4. It is good but fair, because 3 power is easy to block and kill. Each attack swings the life totals while it still guards the gate. The flavour, wounds taken for the weak counted as prayer, reflects the Catholic teaching that suffering can be offered up.

**Bell of Vespers** · Uncommon · Relic · 2W · At the beginning of your upkeep, gain 2 life.
Slow, steady life gain: 2 per turn for 3 mana does nothing to the board, so it is a sideboard or grindy card rather than a staple. It makes racing hopeless for aggressive decks. Vespers is the evening hour of the Office. The line about the restless dead recalls the tolling of bells for the dead and the Vespers of the Office of the Dead.

**Litany of the Faithful** · Uncommon · Spell (Blessing) · 2W · Creatures you control get +1/+2 until end of turn.
A team trick that leans to defence. The extra toughness wins combats and blowouts rather than pushing lethal damage. It is fair for 3 mana at uncommon. A litany is a call-and-response prayer, like the Litany of the Saints, and the flavour (one voice begins, a hundred answer) describes it accurately.

**Abbess of the Silent Order** · Rare · Creature (Human Cleric) · 2WW · 2/4 · When this enters, gain 3 life. When this dies, creatures you control get +1/+1 permanently.
A build-around for go-wide Church decks. She stabilises when she arrives, and her death makes the whole army stronger, so opponents do not want to kill her. That is martyrdom at its strongest, recalling Tertullian's "the blood of the martyrs is the seed of the Church". The silent order evokes Carthusian or Trappist silence.

**Seraph of the Dawn Watch** · Rare · Creature (Angel) · 2WW · 3/4 · Flying, vigilance.
A 4-mana 3/4 flier with vigilance is a strong, simple rare that attacks and still guards against other fliers. The WW in the cost keeps it in heavy-white decks. Seraphim are the highest of the nine angelic choirs, and a seraph keeping watch over the cathedral since before its first stone is a lovely image of guardian angels.

**Reliquary of the First Martyr** · Rare · Relic · 3W · At the beginning of your upkeep, target creature you control gets +1/+1 permanently.
A slow engine. If left alone it turns any creature into a threat, so it rewards board presence. It is strong in long games and weak when you are behind, which is right for a rare. A reliquary holds a first-class relic, the bodily remains of a saint, and the flavour (a single bone, and it is enough) captures the Church's veneration of relics well.

**Archangel of the Last Vigil** · Mythic · Creature (Angel) · 4WW · 5/5 · Flying, lifelink. When this enters, destroy target enemy creature with power 4 or less and gain 4 life.
The Church's finisher, and very strong. A 5/5 flying lifelinker alone is already a top-tier 6-drop, and the removal plus 4 life is a huge swing. If it dominates, change the removal to "power 3 or less" or drop the extra life. The Vigil, the night watch before a great feast, and the image of the last candle make him the light that endures. The name naturally recalls St Michael.

## The Black Covenant (B)

**Covenant Initiate** · Common · Creature (Human Cultist) · B · 1/1 · When this dies, the enemy player loses 1 life and you gain 1 life.
A one-drop chump blocker that drains when it dies. It is weak in stats but always worth something, and it feeds sacrifice and drain decks. The flavour shows the Covenant's logic: even a follower's death belongs to his master.

**Blood-Oath Thrall** · Common · Creature (Human Cultist) · 1B · 3/2 · When this enters, you lose 1 life.
A 3/2 for 2 is above par, paid for with 1 life, the Covenant's bargain at its simplest. It is a good aggressive common. The fingertip of blood and the debt that "came due later" make clear that the deal is a trap.

**Tomb Crawler** · Common · Creature (Zombie) · 1B · 1/1 · Deathtouch.
A cheap deterrent: nothing big wants to attack into it. It is fair at 2 mana, since deathtouch makes up for the tiny body. It shows the Covenant's habit of destroying what is greater than itself.

**Grave Chanter** · Common · Creature (Human Cultist) · 2B · 2/3 · When this enters, the enemy player loses 1 life and you gain 1 life.
An on-rate 2/3 for 3 with a small drain, a steady common that slowly tips races. Singing hymns backwards is the traditional inversion of sacred worship that marks the diabolic, making plain what this faith is.

**Gloam Shade** · Common · Creature (Spirit) · 2B · 2/2 · Flying.
The Covenant's common flier, the exact counterpart of the Church's Herald. Matching rates across faiths keeps the commons fair. A soul-shadow that "stopped caring" hints at the despair that follows damnation.

**Ossuary Ghoul** · Common · Creature (Zombie) · 3B · 3/3 · When this dies, create a 1/1 Restless Bones token.
A 3/3 for 4 is a bit under par, made up for by the token it leaves behind, so it trades for two cards. It is a fine common for grindy decks and introduces the "raising the dead" theme gently.

**Hellmaw Hound** · Common · Creature (Demon Hound) · 3B · 4/3 · (no text)
A vanilla 4/3 for 4, an on-rate attacker that keeps the faith from being all tricks. It is plain and honest, the way a common should be. Its leash forged from screams keeps the demonic flavour unpleasant, as it should be.

**Wither the Living** · Common · Spell (Curse) · 1B · Destroy target enemy creature with power 2 or less.
Cheap, efficient removal. It is slightly better per mana than the Church's Word of Banishment but has a narrower target range. Destruction is the Covenant's specialty, so it deserves the most efficient removal. It could cost 1 life to fit the "sin costs" theme, but it is fine as it is.

**Siphon of Ashes** · Common · Spell (Curse) · 2B · The enemy player loses 3 life and you gain 3 life.
A 6-point life swing for 3 mana. It is good reach for drain decks and useless for board control, which is a fair trade. The cracked chalice deliberately profanes a sacred vessel, a sign of the Covenant's mockery of holy things.

**Veiled Blade of the Covenant** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Assassin) · 2B · 2/2 · Deathtouch. When this dies, the enemy player loses 2 life and you gain 2 life.
A deathtouch 2/2 for 3 that drains 2 when it dies. Blocking it or killing it both cost the opponent. That is a strong uncommon and the core of the faith's "you always pay" identity. The flavour (paid in years stolen from victims) is suitably sinister.

**Cinder-Pit Fiend** · Uncommon · Creature (Demon) · 2BB · 4/4 · At the beginning of your upkeep, you lose 1 life.
A 4/4 for 4 is slightly above par, taxed by a life drain every turn. It rewards aggressive Covenant players and punishes those who keep it too long, which is exactly the lesson: the demon serves but is always hungry.

**Grave Summoner** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Necromancer) · 4B · 2/3 · When this enters, return your best creature card from your graveyard to play.
Value reanimation. A weak 2/3 for 5, but it can bring back a big threat. Its strength depends on the graveyard: superb late, poor early. If the "best creature" picker returns mythics too reliably, raise the cost or limit it to creatures with power 4 or less. "Your rest was never part of the bargain" shows the Covenant's contempt for the dead.

**Dread Whisper** · Uncommon · Spell (Curse) · 1B · The enemy discards 2 cards at random.
Discard as card advantage, a clean two-for-one for 2 mana. Comparable MTG cards cost 3 mana, so this is a little strong, but random discard and the uncommon rarity soften it. Undoing plans with a whisper fits the tempter's work.

**Chalice of Black Vows** · Uncommon · Relic · 2B · At the beginning of your upkeep, the enemy player loses 1 life and you gain 1 life.
A drain clock: 2 points of swing per turn for 3 mana, slow but relentless. It is the evil mirror of the Bell of Vespers, and the comparison is deliberate: one blesses, the other steals. The chalice "never empty" inverts the eucharistic cup.

**Marquis of the Wailing Pit** · Rare · Creature (Demon) · 2BB · 5/5 · Flying. When this enters, you lose 4 life.
A 5/5 flier for 4 mana is far above par, and 4 life is the price. It is a strong, aggressive rare and maybe a little too efficient: 4 life is cheap when you are ahead. Raise the cost to 6 life or 3BB if it dominates. "Presents his bill at the door" is the whole Covenant in one line. Marquis is a rank borrowed from old demonologies.

**Hollow-Crowned Lich** · Rare · Creature (Lich) · 3BB · 3/5 · Deathtouch. At the beginning of your upkeep, the enemy player loses 1 life and you gain 1 life.
A durable defensive engine: a deathtouch wall that drains every turn. It is about par in stats with a strong upside, which suits a rare. The hollow crown is a fitting picture of what sin pays: everything traded, nothing gained.

**Unmaking Rite** · Rare · Spell (Curse) · 2B · Destroy target enemy creature. You lose 2 life.
Unconditional removal for 3 mana and 2 life. It is efficient but comparable to MTG commons, so rare may be too high. It is a good candidate to move to uncommon as the set grows. "Every unmaking costs the maker a little of his own soul" states the faith's rule directly.

**Belzareth, Prince of Cinders** · Mythic · Creature (Demon Lord) · 4BB · 5/5 · Flying. When this enters, destroy target enemy creature. When this dies, the enemy player loses 3 life and you gain 3 life.
The faith's bomb. Unconditional removal plus a 5/5 flier plus value on death is more than the Church's Archangel delivers, and it may be the strongest card in the set. Charging life when he enters (say, 3) would both balance him and fit the theme better. The name echoes Beelzebub, and his presence blights the earth, so he is shown as plainly evil.

## The Old Ways (G)

**Briar Fox** · Common · Creature (Beast) · G · 2/1 · (no text)
A vanilla 2/1 for 1, the Old Ways' cheap early play. It is on par and forgettable by design. The fox slipping through the hedge is pure English countryside.

**Hedgerow Druid** · Common · Creature (Human Druid) · 1G · 1/2 · At the beginning of your upkeep, add G to your mana pool this turn.
The common ramp creature. It gives an extra mana from the next turn on, so turn-2 Druid leads to a turn-3 four-drop. That is the faith's core engine and is fair at 2 mana. A druidess coaxing growth from the soil is a traditional role and a gentle portrayal of paganism.

**Thornback Boar** · Common · Creature (Beast) · 1G · 3/2 · (no text)
A 3/2 for 2 is above par in vanilla stats, the faith's reward for having no tricks: bigger bodies for the cost. It is a solid common.

**Oakmoot Sentinel** · Common · Creature (Treefolk) · 2G · 2/4 · Reach.
A defensive three-drop that answers fliers, the Old Ways' answer to the Church and Covenant air forces. It is about par. A moot is the old assembly of the land, which suits a tree that "saw no need to leave".

**Antlered Barrow-Stag** · Common · Creature (Beast) · 2G · 3/3 · Trample.
An on-rate 3/3 with trample, a good common that stops chump blocks. Barrow-mounds and sleeping kings call up the prehistoric landscape the Old Ways come from.

**Barrowland Bear** · Common · Creature (Beast) · 3G · 4/4 · (no text)
A vanilla 4/4 for 4, an honest common at par. The druids calling her "grandmother" adds warmth to the pagan faith without making it a rival to the Church.

**Call of the Pack** · Common · Spell (Rite) · 2G · Create two 2/2 Wolf tokens.
Four power across two bodies for 3 mana is efficient, and it builds the "many" side of the faith. It is fair at common, and good with Stampede.

**Wildsurge** · Common · Spell (Rite) · G · Target creature you control gets +3/+3 until end of turn.
The classic green trick, +3/+3 for 1 mana. It is fair and essential, because with no removal in the faith, winning combats is how the Old Ways handle threats.

**Greening Rite** · Common · Spell (Rite) · G · Add GG to your mana pool this turn.
One-shot acceleration, netting one mana and costing a card. It is fine at common and lets a turn-3 four-drop happen. The flavour (seed into soil, sap into power) states nature's cycle as the faith's theology.

**Wolfmother of the Fen** · Uncommon · Creature (Beast) · 2G · 2/2 · When this enters, create a 2/2 Wolf token.
Two 2/2s for 3 mana, stronger than Call of the Pack because one of them is a real creature card. Good at uncommon. She "never runs alone", and the pack expresses abundance.

**Elder of the Oak Circle** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Druid) · 3G · 2/3 · When this enters, create two 1/1 Oakling tokens.
Three bodies for 4 mana. The power is spread thin, but it is good with team pumps. A druidess planting acorns that walk is the sort of folk magic that makes the faith charming.

**Stonehide Spirit** · Uncommon · Creature (Spirit) · 2GG · 3/5 · Reach.
A big defensive body with reach, above par on toughness. It stops fliers and most ground attackers. "Not empty, only patient" suggests the spirits pagan peoples believed lived in the stones.

**Tusked Juggernaut** · Uncommon · Creature (Beast) · 4G · 5/5 · Trample.
A 5/5 trampler for 5, an on-rate top-end threat. Fair, and a good target for ramp. Palisades and siege towers as "underbrush" shows nature's indifference to human works.

**Circle of Standing Stones** · Uncommon · Relic · 3G · At the beginning of your upkeep, add GG to your mana pool this turn.
Heavy ramp: +2 mana per turn from 4 mana. That is a lot at uncommon, and with Archdruid it gives explosive starts. Consider rare, or reduce to a single G. The stones "aligned to no star anyone can name" evoke Stonehenge-style mystery.

**Mistletoe Archdruid** · Rare · Creature (Human Druid) · 2G · 2/3 · At the beginning of your upkeep, add GG to your mana pool this turn.
A turn-3 play that adds 2 mana every turn after is very strong: turn-4 six-drops. It is the clearest balance concern in the faith, and 3G or a single G would be safer. Gathering mistletoe with a golden sickle is the druidic rite described by Pliny, a well-chosen historical detail.

**Antler-King of the Fens** · Rare · Creature (Beast Spirit) · 3GG · 5/5 · Trample, vigilance.
A 5/5 for 5 with two keywords is strong but fair for a rare. It attacks without leaving you open. The twelve-tined crown makes it a sovereign of the marsh, a pagan image of nature's majesty.

**Stampede of the Wild Hunt** · Rare · Spell (Rite) · 2GG · Creatures you control get +2/+2 until end of turn.
A team-pump finisher, best after Call of the Pack or Elder. Fair at 4 mana, though trample would make it a real game-ender. The Wild Hunt is the European folk myth of a spectral hunting party, a fitting name.

**Cernoth, the Thousand-Year Oak** · Mythic · Creature (Treefolk Spirit) · 4GGG · 7/7 · Trample, reach. When this enters, create two 2/2 Oakling tokens.
The ramp payoff: 11 power across three bodies. Huge, but it costs 7 and has no protection, which is fair for a mythic the faith is built to reach early. The flavour (empires rose and fell in a single ring) captures nature's slow timescale.

## The Revel (R)

**Tavern Brawler** · Common · Creature (Human Warrior) · R · 2/1 · (no text)
A 2/1 for 1, the Revel's cheapest attacker. On par. The toast and the stool sum up the faith: enjoyment that ends in violence.

**Vine-Crowned Bacchant** · Common · Creature (Human Bacchant) · 1R · 2/1 · Haste.
A hasty 2/1 for 2, slightly under par in stats to pay for haste. A good common for racing. The Bacchant (from Bacchus) dancing from the wine-press to the battle line is the faith's pagan-classical root.

**Masquerade Duelist** · Common · Creature (Human Duelist) · 1R · 2/2 · Whenever this attacks, it gets +1/+0 until end of turn.
An on-rate 2/2 that attacks as a 3/2. A good aggressive common that rewards attacking every turn. The mask hiding the blade shows the darker side of courtly pleasure.

**Fire-Eater of the Feast** · Common · Creature (Human Performer) · 2R · 2/2 · When this enters, deal 1 damage to any enemy creature or the enemy player.
A 2/2 for 3 with a ping, about par. It kills x/1s, and the faith's flexible burn can go face. The flavour (applause, then silence) is darkly comic, which keeps the Revel "slightly evil" rather than villainous.

**Gluttonous Baron** · Common · Creature (Human Noble) · 2R · 4/3 · When this enters, you lose 2 life.
A 4/3 for 3, a full point above par, paid for in life. The Revel's mirror of the Covenant's Thrall, but here the cost is self-indulgence rather than a pact. The gluttony makes "excess hurts the reveller" concrete.

**Carousing Lancer** · Common · Creature (Human Knight) · 3R · 4/3 · Haste.
A hasty 4/3 for 4, an on-rate surprise attacker and a good common. The knight charging with his goblet unspilt shows the faith's reckless glamour.

**Toast of Fire** · Common · Spell (Revelry) · R · Deal 2 damage to any enemy creature or the enemy player.
The faith's basic burn, 2 damage for 1 mana to a creature or the face. On par and flexible. "To your health!" before lighting the brandy is just the right joke.

**Liquid Courage** · Common · Spell (Revelry) · R · Target creature you control gets +3/+1 until end of turn.
A one-mana offensive trick, mostly power, a little toughness. Weaker than Wildsurge on defence, which is right for an aggressive faith. Three cups for glory: courage that does not last.

**Banquet Flambé** · Common · Spell (Revelry) · 2R · Deal 4 damage to target enemy creature.
The faith's common removal for midsize threats. Creature-only to balance the extra damage. Fair at 3 mana.

**Duelist of the Velvet Court** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Duelist) · 2R · 3/2 · Haste. Whenever this attacks, it gets +1/+1 until end of turn.
A hasty 3/2 that attacks as a 4/3 is strong for 3 mana, perhaps the best uncommon attacker in the set. If it dominates, drop haste or make the bonus +1/+0. "Never lost a duel, a wager, or an argument about wine" is pure Revel.

**Masked Firebreather** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Performer) · 2R · 2/2 · Whenever this attacks, deal 1 damage to any enemy creature or the enemy player.
A repeatable pinger that needs to attack. A fair uncommon: weak stats, strong upside if it survives. Its first attack happens a turn later than the Juggler's, which separates the uncommon from the rare.

**Hierophant of the Bacchanal** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Bacchant) · 3R · 3/3 · Haste. When this enters, creatures you control get +2/+0 until end of turn.
A hasty 3/3 plus a team pump, a turn of all-out attack. Good at 4 mana. A hierophant is a priest of mysteries, and here he leads an orgy of battle. "Tonight there is no tomorrow" is the Epicurean motto pushed to excess.

**Bottomless Wine Cask** · Uncommon · Relic · 2R · At the beginning of your upkeep, draw a card and you lose 1 life.
A card-advantage engine for 3 mana at uncommon is strong, since similar MTG cards are usually rare. But the life loss suits a faith that already pays life. Its flavour ("pleasure that never ends is its own slow wound") is the clearest moral statement in the faith. Move it to rare if it shows up everywhere.

**Pyre-Dancer's Whirl** · Uncommon · Spell (Revelry) · 2R · Deal 2 damage to each enemy creature. You lose 2 life.
A one-sided sweeper. Very strong against tokens (Old Ways, Technocracy servos), so it is good that it costs life. Dancers spinning closer to the fire are the image of excess.

**Salamander Juggler** · Rare · Creature (Human Performer) · 1R · 2/2 · Haste. Whenever this attacks, deal 1 damage to any enemy creature or the enemy player.
A hasty two-drop that deals 3 damage on turn 2. A strong rare at the bottom of the curve and the Revel's build-around for aggression. The "salamander" is the traditional elemental of fire. A juggler handling living flames is a good fantasy image.

**Archduke of the Endless Feast** · Rare · Creature (Human Noble) · 2RR · 5/4 · Haste. At the beginning of your upkeep, you lose 1 life.
A hasty 5/4 for 4 is well above par, taxed by an upkeep drain. Like the Covenant's Fiend, but hasty and more dangerous. Strong but self-defeating in long games, so it is well tuned. The eleven-year banquet and the complaining liver are the Revel's gentle self-mockery.

**Grand Conflagration** · Rare · Spell (Revelry) · 2RR · Deal 5 damage to any enemy creature or the enemy player.
Big flexible burn: kills almost anything or finishes a player. Fair at 4 mana and rare. The unplanned encore after the fireworks is the faith's comic ruin.

**Vessar, Master of the Undying Feast** · Mythic · Creature (Human Noble) · 3RR · 5/5 · Haste. Whenever this attacks, deal 2 damage to any enemy creature or the enemy player. At the beginning of your upkeep, you lose 1 life.
The mythic: a hasty 5/5 that deals 7 on the turn it lands. Very strong, with the upkeep drain as the only check. It is fair because the Revel's own life total is the limit. She is a hostess whose guests "do not rise again", the faith's "slightly evil" side made explicit. She is a noblewoman, not a warrior, as the tone rules require.

## The Stoa (U)

**Owl Familiar of the Archive** · Common · Creature (Bird) · U · 1/1 · Flying.
A 1/1 flier for 1, on par. A chump blocker against fliers and a slow evasive clock. The owl "reading by sitting on books" is gentle scholarly humour.

**Lamp-Lit Scribe** · Common · Creature (Human Scholar) · 1U · 1/2 · When this enters, draw a card.
A two-drop that replaces itself, the classic blue value creature. Fair at 2 mana. Copying text changes the copier, which echoes the monastic scriptoria where learning was preserved.

**Bronze-Shield Hoplite** · Common · Creature (Human Soldier) · 1U · 1/4 · Defender.
A cheap wall that stops early aggression. Good for 2 mana, and defender keeps it honest. "What is in my power is to hold this ground" is straight Epictetus: the Stoic distinction between what is in our power and what is not.

**Disciplined Phalanx** · Common · Creature (Human Soldier) · 2U · 2/4 · Vigilance.
A defensive three-drop that can still attack safely. On par. "Forty shields, one mind" is the Stoic ideal of the ordered self expressed as formation.

**Tower Stargazer** · Common · Creature (Human Astronomer) · 3U · 2/3 · Flying. When this enters, draw a card.
A 2/3 flier plus a card for 4 mana is fair-to-good value. It gives the Stoa an evasive threat that pays for itself. The silver-and-glass balcony fits the faith's colours.

**Wandering Peripatetic** · Common · Creature (Human Philosopher) · 3U · 3/3 · When this enters, return target enemy creature to its owner's hand.
A tempo creature: an on-rate 3/3 that sets back the opponent's best play. Good at common, since bounce is temporary. "Peripatetic" is the Aristotelian school that taught while walking, a learned joke (opponents "walking home").

**Measured Rebuke** · Common · Spell (Discourse) · 1U · Return target enemy creature to its owner's hand.
Bounce for 2 mana, a little weak, since the MTG standard is 1 mana. It could be 1 mana, or add "draw a card" at its current cost. "Reconsider." is a perfect one-word flavour line.

**Hour of Contemplation** · Common · Spell (Discourse) · 2U · Draw two cards.
Plain card advantage at the standard 3-mana rate. The flavour (stillness, clarity, then the answer) is the Stoic order of thought.

**Binding Argument** · Common · Spell (Discourse) · U · Tap target enemy creature. Draw a card.
A cantrip that removes a blocker or stops an attacker for a turn. Efficient for 1 mana and fine at common, since tapping is temporary. The brute stopping to consider the premise is a good image for reason defeating force.

**Dialectician of the Porch** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Philosopher) · 2U · 2/2 · Whenever this attacks, tap target enemy creature.
An attacker that removes a blocker each attack. On-rate stats with strong pressure. "The Porch" is literally the Stoa, the painted colonnade in Athens where Zeno taught.

**Archive Great-Owl** · Uncommon · Creature (Bird) · 3U · 3/3 · Flying.
A 3/3 flier for 4, a clean evasive threat and slightly above common rate. A fair uncommon, perhaps a little plain for its slot. Could add a small trigger.

**Strategos of the Shieldwall** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Soldier) · 3U · 3/5 · Vigilance. When this enters, tap target enemy creature.
A 3/5 vigilance for 4 is above par, plus a tempo trigger. Strong at uncommon, perhaps the best in the faith. A strategos is a Greek general, which suits the Hellenic flavour of the faith.

**Orrery of Fixed Stars** · Uncommon · Relic · 3U · At the beginning of your upkeep, tap target enemy creature.
A permanent blocker-remover. Because it fires on your upkeep, it stops a blocker every turn but does not stop attacks. Fair at 4 mana. The orrery's "fixed order" evokes the Stoic cosmos governed by reason (logos).

**Stillness of Mind** · Uncommon · Spell (Discourse) · 3U · Tap all enemy creatures. Draw a card.
A finisher disguised as control: clears all blockers for one attack and replaces itself. Strong but fair at 4 mana. An army "sitting down" at a lecture on vanity is a good joke.

**Sage of the Long Patience** · Rare · Creature (Human Philosopher) · 2UU · 2/4 · Vigilance. When this enters, draw two cards.
A sturdy blocker plus two cards for 4 mana is strong value. A good rare and the faith's long-game build-around. "The obstacle is the way" paraphrases Marcus Aurelius (*Meditations* 5.20).

**Skywarden of the Upper Stoa** · Rare · Creature (Bird Spirit) · 3U · 3/4 · Flying, vigilance.
Identical stats to the Church's Seraph but only a single U in its cost, so it is slightly better. Balanced but plain for a rare. Could go to 2UU for parity with the Seraph, or gain a small trigger.

**Codex of the Unshaken Mind** · Rare · Relic · 2UU · At the beginning of your upkeep, draw a card.
An extra card per turn with no drawback for 4 mana. Strong in a long game and does nothing the turn it is played, so it fits the faith. Compare the Revel's Cask: discipline gets the cards without the wound, and that is the moral point.

**Aristeon, the Unmoved Sage** · Mythic · Creature (Human Philosopher) · 4UU · 4/5 · Flying, vigilance. When this enters, return target enemy creature to its owner's hand and draw two cards.
The control mythic: a sturdy evasive body, a tempo swing and two cards. Very strong, but bounce is temporary and 4 power is a slow clock, so it is weaker than the Archangel or Belzareth. The name means "best", and the "Unmoved" title calls up Stoic *apatheia*, a philosopher so free of passion that he "kept rising".

## Notes and possible adjustments

- **Mythic power gap.** Belzareth and the Archangel (removal on a 5/5 flier) far outclass the other four mythics. Give Belzareth a life cost when he enters, and limit the Archangel to power 3.
- **Ramp density.** Archdruid, Circle of Standing Stones, Brass Dynamo and Perpetual Engine together allow turn-4 six-drops. Archdruid should make one G, Circle should move to rare, and Dynamo to uncommon.
- **The Old Ways have no removal.** That is by design, but it pushes them toward colourless removal. Consider a "fight" action or a spell that only hits fliers.
- **Colourless auto-includes.** Galvanic Coil, Dynamo and Perpetual Engine fit any deck too easily and blur faith identities.
- **The Stoa's tap effects are offensive.** They mostly remove blockers, and the engine has no countermagic, so "defensive tempo" is thin.
- **Bounce is under-rate.** Measured Rebuke should cost U.
- **Unmaking Rite** is common-level power at rare; move it to uncommon as the set grows.
- **Rare filler.** Quicksilver Lancer, Colossus and Skywarden are plain for rares.
- **Curve.** Every faith has 2 or 3 two-drop creatures and a crowded 4-slot. The Church, the Revel and the Stoa each have only one card costing 5 or more.
