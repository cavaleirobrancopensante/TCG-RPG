## The Stoa (U)

The Stoa plays the long game. It draws and sorts its cards, taps, freezes and bounces the enemy to buy time, and wins with sturdy vigilant soldiers and philosophers who never have to choose between attacking and guarding. Its Composure payoffs reward the player who holds four or more cards instead of emptying his hand, and it has no damage, no destruction and no life gain: only patience.

**Owl of the Archive** · Common · Creature (Bird) · U · 1/1 · When this enters, foresight 2 (look at the top two cards of your library, put any of them back on top and the rest on the bottom).
A one-drop that smooths your next draws. The set 1 owl lost its wings with the Stoa's flying, so it becomes what owls in a library really are: quiet watchers. A 1/1 for U with a small foresight is fair, and it gives the faith an early play that is not just a body.

**Ephebe of the Phalanx** · Common · Creature (Human Hoplite) · U · 2/1 · Unmoved (enemy spells and abilities can't tap it, return it to hand or shrink it).
A 2/1 for one with unmoved, an on-rate first play that the enemy's tricks cannot tap, bounce or shrink out of the line; only a fair fight removes it. It is the smallest lesson in the faith's refusal to be moved. The ephebes were young Athenians in military training, and their oath really did promise not to abandon a comrade in the line.

**Water-Carrier Pupil** · Common · Creature (Human Philosopher) · 1U · 1/2 · As long as you have four or more cards in hand (composure), this gets +1/+1.
The common introduction to Composure: a 1/2 for two that becomes a 2/3 while you hold your cards. It teaches the Stoa player early that patience is paid. Cleanthes, Zeno's successor, famously worked nights drawing water to pay for his lessons.

**Lamp-Lit Scribe** · Common · Creature (Human Scholar) · 1U · 1/2 · When this enters, draw a card.
The classic two-drop that replaces itself, kept from set 1 because it is exactly right. A 1/2 plus a card for two mana is fair and keeps the hand full for Composure. Scribes and copied scrolls are how the Stoa's teaching survives.

**Bronze-Shield Hoplite** · Common · Creature (Human Hoplite) · 1U · 2/4 · Defender (can't attack).
The faith's cheap wall: a 2/4 defender for two stops most early attackers cold and kills the small ones. Defender is a real drawback, so the extra stats are earned. The headland line is from the Meditations (4.49), the Stoic image of steadiness under constant assault.

**Logician of the Porch** · Common · Creature (Human Philosopher) · 1U · 1/3 · Tap, pay U: tap target enemy creature.
The common tapper. A modest 1/3 for two whose ability removes a blocker every turn, at the price of mana and its own attack. Because abilities work in your main phase, it is an offensive tool that helps your vigilant bodies get through. "If it is day, it is light" is the standard example in Chrysippus's logic.

**Disciplined Phalanx** · Common · Creature (Human Hoplite) · 2U · 2/4 · Vigilance (doesn't tap to attack).
The Stoa's signature common body: it attacks and still stands guard. A 2/4 vigilance for three is a point over the vanilla rate, which is acceptable because two power is a slow clock and the faith has no other way to win. Kept from set 1.

**Tower Stargazer** · Common · Creature (Human Astronomer) · 2U · 2/2 · When this enters, foresight 3 (look at the top three cards of your library, put any of them back on top and the rest on the bottom).
A 2/2 for three that sets up the next few turns. Without flying it moves down to three mana and swaps its card for a deeper foresight, which keeps it distinct from the Scribe. Stoic physics held the heavens to be the clearest sign of a rational order, so the astronomer belongs here.

**Wandering Cynic** · Common · Creature (Human Philosopher) · 3U · 3/3 · When this enters, return target enemy creature to its owner's hand.
A tempo creature: an on-rate 3/3 that also sends an enemy creature of your choice back to its owner's hand. Bounce is temporary, so this is fine at common. Renamed from the Peripatetic, because the Stoa descends from the Cynics: Zeno learned from Crates, who owned exactly a cloak, a staff and a satchel.

**Shipwrecked Merchant** · Common · Creature (Human Merchant Sailor) · 3U · 2/4 · When this enters, freeze target enemy creature (tap it; it doesn't untap during its controller's next untap step).
The defensive twin of the Cynic. The freeze on arrival keeps the attacker you pick home for a turn, and the 2/4 body holds the ground afterwards. Zeno was a merchant whose cargo sank off Athens; he wandered into a bookshop and found philosophy instead. In 1.25 he became a Sailor too, since a trader who ships his own purple dye across the Aegean has spent his life at sea.

**Veteran of the Long Walls** · Common · Creature (Human Hoplite) · 3U · 3/5 · Unmoved (enemy spells and abilities can't tap it, return it to hand or shrink it).
The common top end. A 3/5 with unmoved for four gives up a point of power so that no enemy trick can tap, bounce or shrink it; it has to be beaten in a fair fight, and five toughness makes that hard. It gives the faith a sturdy body to anchor the line. The Long Walls joined Athens to its harbour, and the line is from the Discourses (1.24).

**Measured Rebuke** · Common · Spell (Discourse) · U · Return target enemy creature to its owner's hand.
Plain bounce, now at one mana as the set 1 notes asked. It saves a turn against a big threat or clears a blocker for the vigilant line. The one-word flavour is the faith's whole manner.

**Binding Argument** · Common · Spell (Discourse) · 1U · Tap target enemy creature. Draw a card.
A cantrip that removes a blocker for a turn. It moved up to two mana because a free tap plus a card at one mana outclassed the rest of the commons. Reason halting force for a moment is the Stoa in one image.

**Grip of the Cold Sea** · Common · Spell (Discourse) · 1U · Freeze target enemy creature (tap it; it doesn't untap during its controller's next untap step). If you control a Sailor, draw a card.
A freeze for the fleet (revised in 1.27 with the tribe condition). Cast it on your turn and the target can neither block now nor attack on the enemy's turn; with a Sailor in play it also replaces itself. It went up a mana for the card, so outside the tribe it is a little worse than before and inside it a match for Binding Argument. The cold silver sea is part of the faith's palette.

**Reading by Lamplight** · Common · Spell (Meditation) · U · Draw a card, then foresight 3 (look at the top three cards of your library, put any of them back on top in any order and the rest on the bottom).
Card selection for one mana: draw, then set the next three cards in order. It replaces itself, so it never costs Composure. Seneca's second letter warns against skimming many books instead of reading a few well, which is what foresight does with your library.

**Hour of Contemplation** · Common · Spell (Meditation) · 2U · Draw two cards.
Plain draw two for three, the standard rate from the spec. It is the faith's simplest way to reach four cards in hand. Kept from set 1.

**Question of First Principles** · Common · Spell (Discourse) · 1U · Tax 2 (the enemy's next non-land card costs 2 more). Draw a card.
The common tax spell. It replaces itself and slows the enemy's next play by two mana, which can push a big creature back a whole turn. Stoic logic began every argument by fixing its terms, a habit that maddens men in a hurry.

**Sent Back to the Gate** · Common · Spell (Discourse) · 2U · Return target enemy creature to its owner's hand. Tax 1 (the enemy's next non-land card costs 1 more).
Bounce with a sting: the enemy has to recast the creature and pay one more for it. At three mana it costs more than the Rebuke but gains a real extra turn of tempo. The quotation is from On Anger, and delay is exactly what this card sells.

**Scholar's Lamp** · Common · Relic (Relic) · U · At the beginning of your upkeep, foresight 1 (look at the top card of your library; you may put it on the bottom).
A tiny engine: it improves every draw for the rest of the duel. It does nothing to the board, so one mana is right. Lamps and night study are the faith's quiet imagery.

**Harbour Chain** · Common · Relic (Relic) · 1U · Tap, pay 2: tap target enemy creature.
A cheap relic with an expensive ability, so it costs you tempo every time you use it. It gives decks without a Logician a repeatable way to clear a blocker. Greek harbours really were closed with chains.

**Agora Inspector** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Official) · 1U · 1/3 · Vigilance. Whenever this attacks, tax 1 (the enemy's next non-land card costs 1 more).
A two-drop that taxes the enemy each time it attacks, and vigilance lets it do so without leaving you open. A 1/3 is hard to block profitably early but never a real threat. The agoranomoi were the officials who checked weights and measures in Greek markets.

**Dialectician of the Porch** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Philosopher) · 2U · 2/2 · Whenever this attacks, tap target enemy creature.
An attacker that removes a blocker every time it attacks, so it protects itself and the rest of the line. On-rate stats for three. "The Porch" is the Stoa Poikile in Athens, where Zeno taught.

**Disciple of Chrysippus** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Philosopher) · 2U · 2/2 · As long as you have four or more cards in hand, this gets +1/+1 and has unmoved.
The uncommon Composure payoff: a plain 2/2 that becomes an unmoved 3/3 while you hold your cards. It rewards the deck for drawing rather than dumping its hand. The quotation is the ancient verdict on Chrysippus, who systematised the school.

**Unbroken Shieldwall** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Hoplite) · 2U · 1/6 · Defender. Unmoved.
A wall built to outlast: a 1/6 defender that the enemy cannot tap, bounce or shrink out of the way. Two keywords take it to uncommon, but defender keeps it honest. The flavour is Epictetus's distinction between what is in our power and what is not, set in a soldier's mouth.

**Great-Owl of the Archive** · Uncommon · Creature (Bird) · 3U · 2/3 · Flying. At the beginning of your upkeep, foresight 2.
A slow card quality engine on a body. A 2/3 for four was under rate because the trigger is worth a great deal over a long game; in 1.25 the Stoa was allowed two small Bird fliers, and the owl of Athena takes one of the places, which brings the card up to rate (a 2/3 flier for four, with the engine as the uncommon twist) without making it a threat.

**Strategos of the Shieldwall** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Hoplite) · 3U · 3/3 · Vigilance. Other Hoplite creatures you control get +0/+1.
A small Hoplite lord. The +0/+1 is modest, but on a line of hoplites it puts every wall out of reach of the enemy's removal and blockers. A strategos was a Greek general, elected for his judgement.

**Navarch of the Cold Sea** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Sailor) · 4U · 3/5 · When this enters, freeze target enemy creature.
A five-drop that takes an enemy creature of your choice out of two turns and leaves a sturdy 3/5 behind. Slightly under the vanilla rate to pay for the freeze. A navarch commanded a fleet, and the line is from Seneca's Letters (71).

**Considered Reply** · Uncommon · Spell (Discourse) · 2U · Return target enemy creature to its owner's hand. Then, if you have four or more cards in hand, draw a card.
Bounce that turns into a two-for-one when you keep your hand full. It asks the Stoa player to hold cards rather than play everything. The quotation paraphrases the Enchiridion (33).

**Humbling Lesson** · Uncommon · Spell (Discourse) · 1U · Return target enemy creature with power 4 or greater to its owner's hand. Draw a card.
A cheap answer to the big threats the Stoa cannot destroy, with a card attached. The power restriction keeps it from being better than the Rebuke in every case. Marcus used the king and the mule driver to humble his own pride (Meditations 6.24).

**Letters to Lucilius** · Uncommon · Spell (Meditation) · 3U · Draw two cards, then foresight 3 (look at the top three cards of your library, put any of them back on top in any order and the rest on the bottom).
One mana more than the Hour of Contemplation for a deep look after drawing. It draws two and then sets the next three in order, so the turns after it are the right cards rather than any cards. Seneca's moral letters to Lucilius are the most widely read Stoic book, and this is their first piece of advice.

**Stillness of Mind** · Uncommon · Spell (Meditation) · 3U · Tap all enemy creatures. Draw a card.
The finishing blow for a patient deck: every blocker taps and your vigilant line walks in. It replaces itself, but it does nothing on defence, so four mana is fair. Kept from set 1.

**Orrery of Fixed Stars** · Uncommon · Relic (Relic) · 3U · At the beginning of your upkeep, tap target enemy creature.
A repeatable tap that clears one blocker every turn without costing mana after it lands. It is slow and does nothing on defence, which keeps it at uncommon. Stoic cosmology saw the turning heavens as reason made visible.

**Sumptuary Tablet** · Uncommon · Relic (Relic) · 2U · At the beginning of your upkeep, tax 1 (the enemy's next non-land card costs 1 more).
A steady drag on the enemy's development: every turn, the first card the enemy plays costs one more. It never wins alone, but it buys the slow deck time. Sumptuary laws limited luxury and display, a very Stoic kind of statute.

**Keeper of the Stacks** · Rare · Creature (Human Scholar) · 2U · 1/4 · Defender. Tap, pay 2: draw a card.
The faith's repeatable draw engine on a body, slow on purpose: three mana and a tap for each card. A 1/4 defender also holds the ground while it works. Rare, as the spec asks for repeatable engines.

**Epictetus, the Unbroken** · Rare · Creature (Human Philosopher) · 1UU · 3/3 · Unmoved. As long as you have four or more cards in hand, other creatures you control have unmoved.
The unmoved build-around. A 3/3 for three is an on-rate body, and with Composure your whole army becomes proof against the enemy's taps, bounces and shrinking curses, which blunts tempo decks and the Pact's removal alike. The story of the leg comes from Origen and is the purest picture of a man whom pain cannot move.

**Sage of the Long Patience** · Rare · Creature (Human Philosopher) · 2UU · 2/4 · Vigilance. When this enters, draw two cards.
A sturdy vigilant body plus two cards for four mana: strong value and an easy way into Composure. Kept from set 1 as the faith's long-game card. The line is Meditations 5.20.

**Seneca, Tutor of the Prince** · Rare · Creature (Human Philosopher) · 2UU · 2/3 · At the beginning of your upkeep, if you have four or more cards in hand, draw a card.
The Composure engine: keep four cards and he draws you an extra one every turn. The 2/3 body is weak for four mana, so he must be protected, and an enemy who forces you to empty your hand turns him off. Seneca tutored Nero, and the line opens his first letter to Lucilius.

**Aristeon, the Unmoved Sage** · Rare · Creature (Human Philosopher) · 4UU · 4/5 · Vigilance. When this enters, return target enemy creature to its owner's hand and draw two cards.
The set 1 mythic, now a rare without flying. A vigilant 4/5 is under rate for six, but the bounce and two cards make it a huge tempo and card swing. His name means "the best", and "Unmoved" is Stoic apatheia.

**Apatheia** · Rare · Spell (Meditation) · 2UU · Freeze all enemy creatures (tap them; they don't untap during their controller's next untap step).
The faith's great tempo swing: the enemy can't block this turn, can't attack on their turn, and is still tapped on your next turn. It deals no damage itself, so it only wins with a board already in place. Apatheia is the Stoic ideal of freedom from disordered passion.

**All Things Return** · Rare · Spell (Meditation) · 3UU · Return all creatures to their owners' hands.
A symmetrical reset. The Stoa benefits more than most: its creatures have useful enter triggers, its hand grows toward Composure, and the enemy loses tokens for good. It is fair at five because you lose your board too. The flavour is the Stoic doctrine of the cosmic cycle.

**Codex of the Unshaken Mind** · Rare · Relic (Relic) · 2UU · At the beginning of your upkeep, draw a card.
An extra card every turn with no drawback, slow because it does nothing the turn it lands. Kept from set 1. It is discipline's answer to the Revel's cask: the cards without the wound.

**The Painted Porch** · Rare · Relic (Relic) · 2U · Creatures you control have vigilance.
The vigilance build-around: every creature you control attacks and still guards. It lets the walls of the faith join the attack without leaving you open, though defenders still cannot attack. The Stoa Poikile was decorated by Polygnotus, Micon and Panaenus with famous battles, Marathon and the fall of Troy among them.

**Zeno of Citium** · Mythic · Creature (Human Philosopher) · 2UU · 3/4 · Vigilance. Other Philosopher creatures you control get +1/+1. When this enters, draw a card.
The founder as a Philosopher lord. A vigilant 3/4 that draws a card is already good for four, and every pupil, sage and logician becomes a real threat beside him. The saying is attributed to Zeno by Diogenes Laertius.

**Marcus Aurelius** · Mythic · Creature (Human Noble Philosopher) · 4UU · 5/6 · Vigilance, unmoved. Whenever this attacks, freeze target enemy creature.
The emperor who wrote the Meditations in camp on the Danube. A 5/6 with vigilance and unmoved is a wall that attacks and that no trick can tap, bounce or shrink, and each attack takes an enemy creature of your choice out of two turns. It is splashy, but it is still one creature that destroy effects and chump blocks can answer. The line is Meditations 10.16.

**The View from Above** · Mythic · Spell (Meditation) · 3UU · Draw three cards, then foresight 5. Tax 2 (the enemy's next non-land card costs 2 more).
The Stoa's great sorcery: the deepest foresight in the faith, three cards, and a tax that slows the enemy's answer. It touches no creature, so it cannot win alone, but it fills the hand for every Composure payoff. The view from above is a meditation Marcus returns to again and again (9.30).

### Tribal cards (1.23)

The Stoa's two tribes play the same patient game from two sides. **Hoplites** are the wall: tough, vigilant bodies that grow stronger the more of them stand together (kinship, the Polemarch and Strategos, Trophy of Marathon) and that become a line which attacks without ever leaving the gate open. **Philosophers** are the engine: each one that enters sorts the library, draws cards or makes the next Philosopher cheaper, which keeps the hand full for Composure. The two meet in Socrates and Xenophon, both men who carried the shield and the argument, so a mixed deck is rewarded rather than split. The phalanx soldiers of the first 45 cards (the Ephebe, the Bronze-Shield Hoplite, the Disciplined Phalanx, the Veteran of the Long Walls, the Unbroken Shieldwall and the Strategos) are now Human Hoplites, and the Strategos's lord bonus follows them; Measured Rebuke and Grip of the Cold Sea become instants, since a bounce or a freeze is most at home in answer to an attack.

**Spear of the Second Rank** · Common · Creature (Human Hoplite) · 1U · 1/3 · This gets +1/+0 for each other Hoplite you control.
The common kinship member: a modest blocker alone, a real attacker in a full line. It teaches that Hoplites want company. In the phalanx the second rank's spears reached past the front rank's shoulders, so the man who could not see the enemy still struck him.

**Pupil of the Painted Porch** · Common · Creature (Human Philosopher) · U · 1/1 · Whenever another Philosopher enters under your control, foresight 1 (look at the top card of your library; you may put it on the bottom).
A one-drop that pays off over the whole game: every later Philosopher smooths a draw. The body is tiny, as a pupil should be, and foresight 1 is the gentlest version of the trigger. Students of the Porch really did fetch and carry for their masters, as Cleanthes did.

**Hoplite of Plataea** · Common · Creature (Human Hoplite) · 3U · 3/4 · Vigilance (doesn't tap to attack).
A plain top-end Hoplite on rate (a 4/4 for four, with a point traded for vigilance). It gives the tribe a common body that can finish a game. Herodotus tells how the Spartans at Plataea stood under the Persian arrows until the sacrifices were favourable, then advanced.

**Questioner in the Agora** · Common · Creature (Human Philosopher) · 2U · 2/3 · When this enters, tax 1 (the enemy's next non-land card costs 1 more).
A fair three-drop Philosopher with a small sting, so the tribe has a common body that also slows the enemy. It triggers every Philosopher payoff on arrival. Socrates's habit of asking generals to define courage (the Laches) is the model.

**Farmer Called to the Muster** · Common · Creature (Human Hoplite) · 1U · 2/2 · Vigilance (doesn't tap to attack).
The cheap Hoplite: a vigilant 2/2 that fills the line early and feeds kinship and lords. A point over vanilla, which the Stoa's slow clock allows at two mana. The hoplite was a citizen farmer who bought his own panoply, and the bronze cost him dear.

**Lock Shields** · Common · Spell (Discipline) · 1U · Hoplite creatures you control get +0/+2 and gain unmoved until end of turn (enemy spells and abilities can't tap them, return them to hand or shrink them).
The tribe's combat trick: cast in the enemy's attack, it turns a row of blocks into a row of walls, and since 1.27 it also answers a tapper, a bounce or a shrink on the stack by granting unmoved until end of turn. A point of toughness paid for the keyword. Toughness only, so it stays a defensive Stoa card and does not trespass on the Revel's temporary attack bonuses. Thucydides notes that each man sheltered under the shield of the man on his right.

**Morning Lecture** · Common · Spell (Meditation) · U · Foresight 2 for each Philosopher you control (look at that many cards from the top of your library, put any of them back on top and the rest on the bottom), then draw a card.
A cantrip that grows with the school: weak alone, a deep look at the library with three Philosophers. One mana keeps it a filler, not a payoff. The Porch was a public place, and the lectures began early.

**Polemarch of the Phalanx** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Hoplite) · 2U · 2/2 · Other Hoplite creatures you control get +1/+0 and have vigilance (they don't tap to attack).
The Hoplite lord: a fragile 2/2 that turns the line into an attacking force that still guards the gate. Power plus vigilance pairs neatly with the Strategos's +0/+1. The polemarch held the place of honour on the right wing, as Callimachus did at Marathon.

**Lochagos of the File** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Hoplite) · 2U · 2/3 · Whenever another Hoplite enters under your control, tap target enemy creature.
A Hoplite payoff in the faith's own currency: each new man in the line clears a blocker. It rewards playing the tribe on curve and attacking afterwards. A lochagos commanded a file or company of the phalanx.

**Lecture at the Painted Porch** · Uncommon · Spell (Meditation) · 2U · Draw a card for each Philosopher you control.
The Philosopher draw spell: on rate with two, a bargain with three or more, nothing with none. It asks for a board of thinkers before it pays. The painted battles of the Stoa Poikile are behind the master, as on the Zeno card.

**Scholarch of the Porch** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Philosopher) · 2U · 2/3 · Your Philosopher creature cards cost 1 less to play.
The tribal cost cutter, fair on a 2/3 body that does nothing else. It lets the school arrive a turn early and fuels the Pupil, Socrates and Lecture. The scholarch was the head of a school; the Stoa's passed from Zeno to Cleanthes to Chrysippus.

**Othismos** · Uncommon · Spell (Discipline) · 2U · Freeze target enemy creature (tap it; it doesn't untap during its controller's next untap step). Hoplite creatures you control get +0/+2 until end of turn.
A bigger trick for the line: it stops one attacker for two turns and stiffens the blockers. Three mana at instant speed is the price of doing two things. Othismos was the shoving match of two phalanxes once the spears had broken.

**Socrates at Delium** · Rare · Creature (Human Hoplite Philosopher) · 1UU · 2/3 · Unmoved (enemy spells and abilities can't tap it, return it to hand or shrink it). Whenever another Philosopher enters under your control, draw a card.
The bridge between the tribes and the Philosopher draw engine: every thinker who follows him replaces itself. The body is small and must be protected, and the engine needs other Philosophers, so it is slow, as the spec asks. Alcibiades in the Symposium describes Socrates walking out of the rout at Delium so calmly that no enemy dared touch him, which is unmoved exactly.

**Trophy of Marathon** · Rare · Relic (Relic) · 2U · Hoplite creatures you control get +1/+1.
The Hoplite anthem as a relic: harder to remove than a lord and the build-around for a Hoplite deck. It is restricted to the tribe, so it does not tread on the Church's team-wide anthems. A tropaion was set up where the enemy turned (trope) to flee, and the trophy of Marathon was famous for centuries.

**Xenophon of the Ten Thousand** · Mythic · Creature (Human Hoplite Philosopher) · 3UU · 4/5 · Vigilance (doesn't tap to attack). Other Hoplite creatures you control get +1/+1. When this enters, draw a card for each Philosopher you control.
The tribal mythic: a lord for the line and a refill for the school in one body, splashy but still a creature that a destroy or a bounce answers. He counts himself, so he always draws at least one. Xenophon, pupil of Socrates, led the Ten Thousand out of Persia to the Black Sea, where they cried "Thalatta!" at the sight of it.

### Third tribe (1.25)

The Stoa's third tribe is the **Sailors**: the rowers, helmsmen, pilots and admirals of the Athenian fleet. It is small and plays the faith's own game at sea: reading the water (foresight), holding a course (vigilance) and freezing or tapping what stands in the way. Its members are the Oarsman, the Helmsman, the Pilot and Phormio, with the Navarch of the Cold Sea (already a Sailor) and the Shipwrecked Merchant retagged, six in all; the payoffs are modest, a kinship on a one-drop, a foresight trigger on a gull, a vigilance grant, a foresight count and a rare lord. Two Birds now fly, as the spec allows the Stoa at most two small fliers: the new Gull of the Sea Lanes and the Great-Owl of the Archive.

**Oarsman of the Thranite Bench** · Common · Creature (Human Sailor) · U · 1/1 · This gets +1/+0 for each other Sailor you control.
The tribe's one-drop and its kinship member: a 1/1 alone, a real attacker in a full crew. It fills the faith's thin one-drop slot. The thranites rowed on the top bench of a trireme, the hardest and best-paid seats, and the crew of about 170 oars pulled to a single time.

**Gull of the Sea Lanes** · Common · Creature (Bird) · 1U · 1/1 · Flying. Whenever a Sailor enters under your control, foresight 1 (look at the top card of your library; you may put it on the bottom).
One of the Stoa's two fliers: power 1, so it is an evasive chip and a scout, not a threat, and it pays a point of rate (a Church dove is a 1/1 flier for one) for the trigger. It is not a Sailor itself but rewards them. Seamen of every age have read land from the gulls; the temple of Poseidon on Cape Sounion was the Athenian sailor's first sight of home.

**Helmsman of the Paralos** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Sailor) · 2U · 2/3 · Vigilance. Other Sailor creatures you control have vigilance (they don't tap to attack).
The tribe's keyword grant, in the faith's own keyword: the crew attacks and still keeps watch. A vigilant 2/3 for three is fair alone, and the grant is a modest twist rather than a lord's bonus. The Paralos was one of Athens's two sacred state triremes, crewed by free citizens and used for embassies and sacred missions.

**Pilot of the Long Swell** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Sailor) · 2U · 2/3 · When this enters, foresight 2 for each Sailor you control (look at that many cards from the top of your library, put any of them back on top and the rest on the bottom).
The tribe-counting card: counting itself, it always looks at two cards, and in a full crew it sorts much of the library. It draws nothing, so it smooths draws without adding cards, as a Stoa uncommon should. The pilot (kybernetes) was the ship's master of the sea's signs; the long swell that runs ahead of a storm is the oldest of them.

**Phormio of Naupactus** · Rare · Creature (Human Sailor) · 3UU · 3/4 · Other Sailor creatures you control get +1/+1. When this enters, freeze target enemy creature (tap it; it doesn't untap during its controller's next untap step).
The Sailor lord and the tribe's rare: five mana for a 3/4 that freezes on arrival and lifts the crew, so it is slow and still answered by any removal. It does not give the anthem to the whole board, which stays the Church's. Thucydides (2.83 to 2.84) tells how Phormio, in 429 BC, rowed round the Corinthian fleet's defensive ring and waited for the morning wind out of the gulf; when it came the ships fouled one another and he fell on them. The patience is pure Stoa.
