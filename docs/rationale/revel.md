## The Revel (R)

The Revel plays fast and pays for it. Its creatures arrive with haste and first strike, its burn is the only magic in the game that hits a player directly, and its biggest turns come when the party is already going and Carouse switches on. Its new mechanic, Idle, pulls the other way: sleepy nobles, lazy courtiers and lounging giants reward you for leaving them at home, so every turn asks whether to charge or to keep feasting, and every excess costs a little of your own life.

**Tavern Brawler** · Common · Creature (Human Warrior) · R · 2/1 · No abilities.
The honest one-drop: a 2/1 for a single mana, exactly on rate. The Revel needs something to put down on the first turn, and a vanilla body keeps the bottom of the curve clean. A brawler in a tavern is the faith at its most ordinary: pleasure that tips over into a fistfight.

**Vine-Crowned Bacchant** · Common · Creature (Satyr Bacchant) · 1R · 2/1 · Haste (this can attack the turn it comes into play).
A 2/1 with haste for two, a point of toughness under a vanilla 2/2 to pay for the keyword. It is the faith's basic racing card and the first thing that shows how the Revel plays: hit now, worry later. The bacchant is a follower of Bacchus, the classical god of wine, and the vine crown places him in the faith's pagan, classical roots. Since 1.25 he is a satyr, the goat-legged follower of the old wine feasts, and counts for the Satyr tribe.

**Masquerade Duelist** · Common · Creature (Human Duelist) · 1R · 2/1 · First strike (this deals combat damage before creatures without first strike).
The common first striker, a 2/1 for two that wins most early trades and makes x/2 blockers think twice. It is the same price as the Bacchant, so a player picks between speed and swordsmanship. The masked duel of honour at a masquerade is the Revel's courtly face, still pleasure, but pleasure with a rapier in it.

**Courtier on the Chaise** · Common · Creature (Human Noble) · 1R · 0/3 · Idle (at the end of your turn, if this is untapped and didn't attack): deal 1 damage to the enemy player.
The simplest Idle card: a 0/3 wall for two that deals 1 damage to the enemy player every turn it is left alone. It cannot hit creatures, so it is a slow clock and never removal, which keeps it fair at common. The idle courtier lounging on his couch and trading barbs is the faith's laziness made comic, and he does real harm without ever lifting a sword.

**Loafer of the Arbour** · Common · Creature (Human Peasant) · 1R · 1/2 · Idle (at the end of your turn, if this is untapped and didn't attack): rummage (draw a card, then discard a card).
A 1/2 for two that filters your hand every turn it lounges. It is never card advantage, only card quality, which is exactly what rummage is for: the impulsive swap of a bad card for a hopeful one. The loafer under the vines while the harvest waits is the gentle, everyday face of sloth.

**Sleepy Baronet** · Common · Creature (Human Noble) · 2R · 1/3 · Idle (at the end of your turn, if this is untapped and didn't attack): put a +1/+1 counter on this.
The Idle grower. A 1/3 for three is poor on its own, but it becomes a 2/4 at the end of the turn it arrives and keeps growing while it stays home. The tension is the point: every turn you send him in, he stops getting fatter. A minor noble dozing his way to a great belly is the Revel's slow ruin in one picture.

**Fire-Eater of the Feast** · Common · Creature (Human Performer) · 2R · 2/2 · When this enters, deal 1 damage to any enemy creature or the enemy player.
A 2/2 for three that pings on arrival, about par: it kills an x/1 or adds a point of reach. Flexible burn that can go to the face is the Revel's privilege among the faiths. The fire-eater at a banquet is the faith's entertainment, and the flavour keeps its darkness comic.

**Gluttonous Baron** · Common · Creature (Human Noble) · 2R · 4/3 · When this enters, you lose 2 life.
A 4/3 for three, a full point over rate, paid for with 2 of your own life. It is the clearest common example of the faith's excess: more than you should have, at a cost you feel. Gluttony is the oldest of the Revel's vices, and the baron who rises hungry after nine vintages is its comic emblem.

**Masked Reveller** · Common · Creature (Human Reveller) · 2R · 3/2 · Carouse (if you already played another non-land card this turn): this has haste.
A 3/2 for three that gains haste when the party is already going. On its own it is a slightly fragile three-drop; played after a cheap spell or relic it hits at once. It teaches Carouse at common with a single clear reward, and the mask is the faith's carnival emblem.

**Carousing Lancer** · Common · Creature (Human Knight) · 3R · 4/3 · Haste (this can attack the turn it comes into play).
A hasty 4/3 for four, one toughness under a vanilla 4/4 to pay for haste. It is the faith's common surprise attacker at the top of the curve. The knight who charges with his goblet unspilled is the Revel's reckless glamour, foolish and a little magnificent.

**Satyr of the Drowsy Vineyard** · Common · Creature (Satyr) · 3R · 3/3 · Haste (this can attack the turn it comes into play). Idle (at the end of your turn, if this is untapped and didn't attack): put a +1/+1 counter on this.
The common that puts the Idle question most plainly. A 3/3 haste for four is a little under rate, but every turn you hold it back it grows, so each turn you choose between hitting now and hitting harder later. The satyr, the goat-legged companion of Bacchus, asleep among the vines fits the faith's classical roots and its sloth.

**Garlanded Festival Bull** · Common · Creature (Beast) · 4R · 5/4 · When this enters, creatures you control get +1/+0 until end of turn.
The common five-drop: a 5/4, slightly under a vanilla 5/5, that gives the team +1/+0 the turn it lands. The bull itself cannot attack that turn, so the bonus is for the creatures already on the board. The garlanded bull of the festival procession is an old classical and carnival image, and he leads the party into the fight.

**Toast of Fire** · Common · Spell (Revelry) · R · Deal 2 damage to any enemy creature or the enemy player.
The faith's basic burn: 2 damage for one mana to a creature or the face, exactly the common ceiling in the balance guide. It is removal early and reach late. The toast that becomes a flame is the Revel's joke in its simplest form, raised by a lord at his own garden feast.

**Firecracker Garland** · Common · Spell (Revelry) · R · Deal 1 damage to target enemy creature. Carouse (if you already played another non-land card this turn): also deal 1 damage to the enemy player.
A cheap ping that kills x/1s and gets a little better when it is the second card of the turn. It is weaker than Toast of Fire on purpose, and its job is to be the card you play late in a busy turn. Firecrackers strung for a feast day belong to the carnival, and their carelessness is the faith's.

**Spilled Brandy** · Common · Spell (Revelry) · 1R · Deal 1 damage to any enemy creature or the enemy player, then rummage (draw a card, then discard a card).
A ping with a rummage attached: two mana for 1 damage, then a fresh draw and a discard of your choice. It smooths draws and counts toward Carouse, and as an instant it can finish a damaged blocker in the middle of combat. Spilled brandy catching fire is the comic accident that the Revel turns into a weapon.

**Rowdy Chorus** · Common · Spell (Revelry) · 1R · Creatures you control get +1/+0 until end of turn. Carouse (if you already played another non-land card this turn): they get an additional +1/+0 until end of turn.
The common team pump: +1/+0 to everything for two mana, doubled when the party is already going. It is power only and lasts one turn, as the Revel's team bonuses must, so it pushes damage rather than winning combats on defence. A tavern chorus growing louder and less tuneful is the faith's courage in a song.

**Flaming Flagon** · Common · Spell (Revelry) · 1R · Deal 3 damage to the enemy player.
Three damage to the face for two mana, and nothing else. It is a pure finisher, useless against creatures, which is why it gets one more point than Toast of Fire. Only the Revel may hit players directly, and a flagon of burning spirits thrown across the hall is that privilege at its rowdiest.

**Banquet Flambe** · Common · Spell (Revelry) · 2R · Deal 4 damage to target enemy creature.
The faith's common removal for midsize threats: 4 damage for three mana, creature only to pay for the size. It kills nearly every common and most uncommons. A flambe gone wrong is the kitchen side of the feast and keeps the burn comic. (The name drops the accent so it reads cleanly on every screen.)

**Carnival Brazier** · Common · Relic (Relic) · 1R · Tap, pay 1 life: deal 1 damage to the enemy player.
A two-mana relic that trades your life for the enemy's, one point a turn. In a race the Revel is usually ahead, so an even trade favours it, and it is a cheap card for turning on Carouse. The brazier that warms and burns at once is the faith's whole bargain.

**Festival Drum** · Common · Relic (Relic) · 2R · R, Tap: creatures you control get +1/+0 until end of turn.
A repeatable team pump: three mana down, then one mana each turn for +1/+0 across the board before combat. It does nothing alone, which keeps it honest at common, but with a wide board it adds real damage every turn. The festival drum calling the revellers to march is the sound of the faith.

**Duelist of the Velvet Court** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Noble Duelist) · 2R · 2/2 · First strike. Whenever this attacks, it gets +1/+1 until end of turn.
Set 1's strongest attacker, rebuilt. He loses haste and his body drops to 2/2, but he gains first strike, so he attacks as a 3/3 first striker that beats almost every three-drop. He is still the sharpest duelist at uncommon, now without the turn-three surprise. The velvet court is the faith's polished, perfumed nobility, and his only real opponent is the wine list.

**Masked Firebreather** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Performer) · 2R · 2/2 · Whenever this attacks, deal 1 damage to any enemy creature or the enemy player.
A 2/2 for three that pings each time it attacks. Weak stats, strong upside if it survives, and it must attack to work. It is the deliberate mirror of the Jester of the Lazy Court: one burns by going in, the other by staying home.

**Jester of the Lazy Court** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Jester Performer) · 2R · 1/2 · Idle (at the end of your turn, if this is untapped and didn't attack): deal 1 damage to any enemy creature or the enemy player.
The uncommon Idle pinger. Unlike the common Courtier he can aim at creatures, so he clears x/1s and finishes damaged blockers every turn he lounges. A 1/2 body at three mana keeps him fragile and easy to burn. The court jester sprawled on a cushion, throwing sparks instead of rising, is laziness turned to mischief.

**Host of the Lantern Garden** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Noble) · 2R · 0/4 · Idle (at the end of your turn, if this is untapped and didn't attack): put a +1/+0 counter on target creature you control.
The lazy host who feeds the feast. A 0/4 for three that blocks well and gives one of your creatures a permanent +1/+0 at the end of each turn he rests. The bonus is power only, so it fits the Revel's attacking identity and never becomes a Church anthem. He is a nobleman presiding over the garden party, a host and never a fighter, which is why he has no power of his own, and the flavour is Epicurus calling pleasure the beginning and end of the blessed life.

**Fencing Master of the Masque** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Duelist) · 3R · 3/3 · First strike. Carouse (if you already played another non-land card this turn): this has haste.
A 3/3 first striker for four that strikes immediately if it is the second card of the turn. Without the haste it is a solid but unexciting body; with it, it is an uncommon worth building toward. The fencing master of the masked balls joins the faith's two owned combat keywords, first strike and haste.

**Hierophant of the Bacchanal** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Bacchant) · 3R · 3/3 · Haste. When this enters, creatures you control get +1/+0 until end of turn.
A hasty 3/3 that gives the whole team +1/+0 the turn it lands. Set 1's version gave +2/+0, which was too much on a hasty body; +1/+0 still makes it a fine alpha-strike card. A hierophant was a priest of the ancient mysteries, and here he leads the bacchanal into the fight.

**Margrave of the Long Table** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Noble) · 3R · 2/3 · Idle (at the end of your turn, if this is untapped and didn't attack): put two +1/+1 counters on this and you lose 2 life.
The gluttonous Idle grower: a 2/3 for four that becomes a 4/5 at the end of the turn it lands, and keeps swelling as long as you can afford the 2 life. It grows twice as fast as the Sleepy Baronet, and the life loss makes lounging a real cost. A margrave was a lord of the border marches, and this one has abandoned the border for the banquet table.

**Second Bottle** · Uncommon · Spell (Revelry) · R · Deal 1 damage to any enemy creature or the enemy player. Carouse (if you already played another non-land card this turn): also deal 2 damage to the enemy player.
One mana for a ping, three damage in all if the party is already going. It is the uncommon Carouse payoff for burn decks, cheap enough to finish a big turn. The second bottle, opened when good sense has already left, is how the Revel makes poor decisions pay.

**Satisfaction at Dawn** · Uncommon · Spell (Revelry) · 2R · Deal 3 damage to target enemy creature. Carouse (if you already played another non-land card this turn): creatures you control get +1/+0 until end of turn.
Creature removal that turns into an attack spell when played second in the turn. Three damage for three mana is a little under Banquet Flambe, and the Carouse bonus pays for that. The duel of honour at dawn, "satisfaction" in the old phrase, is the faith's courtly violence.

**Pyre-Dancer's Whirl** · Uncommon · Spell (Revelry) · 2R · Deal 2 damage to each enemy creature. You lose 2 life.
A one-sided sweeper that wipes tokens and small creatures, priced at three mana with 2 of your own life. It is very strong against the Old Ways and the Technocracy's servos, which is why the self-wound matters. Dancers whirling ever closer to the fire are the image of excess that does not know when to stop.

**Empty the Cellar** · Uncommon · Spell (Revelry) · 1R · Rummage twice (each time, draw a card, then discard a card). Then deal 2 damage to the enemy player.
Two rummages and 2 damage to the face for two mana. It digs for burn and threats while chipping at the enemy, but never gives an extra card. Emptying the cellar in one night is the impulsive spending of the Revel, the whole inheritance poured out at once.

**Bottomless Wine Cask** · Uncommon · Relic (Relic) · 2R · At the beginning of your upkeep, rummage (draw a card, then discard a card).
Set 1's Cask drew a card every turn, which was too strong for uncommon and is plain card draw the Revel no longer has. Now it rummages every turn: steady card quality, never card advantage. The flavour line remains the clearest moral statement in the faith.

**Carnival Masks** · Uncommon · Relic (Relic) · 1R · Carouse (if you already played another non-land card this turn): creatures you control have first strike.
A two-mana relic that gives the whole team first strike on any turn you have played another non-land card. It rewards busy turns and makes attacks into bigger blockers safe. The carnival mask, which hides who you are and makes you bolder, is the Revel's emblem.

**Salamander Juggler** · Rare · Creature (Human Performer) · 1R · 2/1 · Haste. Whenever this attacks, deal 1 damage to any enemy creature or the enemy player.
A hasty two-drop that pings every time it attacks. Set 1 made it a 2/2, which dealt 3 damage on turn two; as a 2/1 it is still the faith's most dangerous aggressive two-drop but dies to any ping. The salamander is the traditional elemental of fire, and a juggler keeping three of them in the air is a fine carnival image.

**Prince of Idle Hours** · Rare · Creature (Human Noble) · 3R · 2/4 · Idle (at the end of your turn, if this is untapped and didn't attack): deal 2 damage to any enemy creature or the enemy player.
The Idle build-around: a 2/4 for four that deals 2 damage every turn it lounges. That is a lot of repeated damage, so the body is modest and the card is rare, and it dies to any 4-damage spell. A prince who rules from his couch, his courtiers doing his violence, is laziness as a form of power.

**Master of the Midnight Duel** · Rare · Creature (Human Duelist) · 2RR · 3/3 · First strike, haste. Carouse (if you already played another non-land card this turn): this gets +2/+0.
A hasty 3/3 first striker for four, and a 5/3 first striker when the party is going. It is the rare Carouse payoff for the duelists: play a cheap spell, then attack with something no common blocker survives. The duel at midnight after the ball is the faith's courtly violence at its most romantic and most foolish.

**Archduke of the Endless Feast** · Rare · Creature (Human Noble) · 2RR · 5/4 · Haste. At the beginning of your upkeep, you lose 1 life.
A hasty 5/4 for four, well over rate, taxed by a point of life every turn. It wins races quickly and punishes the Revel player who lets the game drag. The eleven-year banquet and the complaining liver are the faith's gentle self-mockery.

**Giant of the Groaning Board** · Rare · Creature (Giant) · 4RR · 6/6 · Idle (at the end of your turn, if this is untapped and didn't attack): put a +1/+1 counter on this, then rummage (draw a card, then discard a card).
The faith's top end: a 6/6 for six that grows and rummages every turn it rests. It is a fair 6/6 that can attack whenever it must, and a slowly swelling engine when it stays home. The groaning board is the old phrase for a table heavy with food, and a giant who never leaves it is gluttony on a heroic scale.

**Grand Conflagration** · Rare · Spell (Revelry) · 2RR · Deal 5 damage to any enemy creature or the enemy player.
Big flexible burn: it kills almost any creature or takes a quarter of a player's life. Four mana and rare keeps it from being a common answer to everything. The unplanned encore after the fireworks is the faith's comic ruin.

**Night Without End** · Rare · Spell (Revelry) · 2RR · Creatures you control get +3/+0 until end of turn. You lose 3 life.
The Revel's finisher: +3/+0 to the whole team for one turn, paid for with 3 life. With three attackers it is nine extra damage, but it does nothing for defence and the life is gone either way. A night that refuses to end is the Epicurean motto, "tonight there is no tomorrow", pushed to its ruinous conclusion.

**Carnival of Cinders** · Rare · Spell (Revelry) · 3RR · Deal 3 damage to each creature and 3 damage to the enemy player. You lose 1 life.
A symmetrical sweeper that also burns the enemy player, at the price of 1 of your own life and your own small creatures. It clears a stalled board when the Revel's quick start has run out, and Idle growers with counters may survive it. The carnival burning down at the end of the season is the faith's slow ruin arriving all at once.

**Lanterns of the Pleasure Garden** · Rare · Relic (Relic) · 2R · Creatures you control have haste.
A three-mana relic that gives every creature you control haste. It is a rare build-around because it changes how the whole deck plays, and it creates the sharpest Idle tension in the set: every creature can attack at once, so every lounger is a choice. The pleasure garden is the Revel's own land, lit for a party that never ends.

**Vessar, Master of the Undying Feast** · Mythic · Creature (Human Noble) · 3RR · 0/5 · Idle (at the end of your turn, if this is untapped and didn't attack): deal 1 damage to each enemy creature and 2 damage to the enemy player.
The Idle mythic: a 0/5 host who sweeps every x/1 and burns the enemy each turn he lounges. He has no power because he never fights; his guests and his wine do the killing. He is answerable by any 5 damage or a determined attack. A lord who poisons his own table is the faith's evil side made plain.

**Brandimarte, Prince of Fire-Eaters** · Mythic · Creature (Human Noble Duelist) · 3RR · 5/4 · Haste, first strike. Whenever this attacks, creatures you control get +1/+0 until end of turn.
The aggressive mythic: a 5/4 with haste and first strike that attacks as a 6/4 and lifts every other attacker by one. It ends games quickly if unanswered, but 4 toughness dies to Banquet Flambe or a blocker plus a ping. The name comes from the knights of Italian romance, and a prince of fire-eaters who leads the charge is the Revel at its most glamorous.

**The Undying Bonfire** · Mythic · Relic (Relic) · 2RR · At the beginning of your upkeep, deal 1 damage to each creature and 1 damage to the enemy player, and you lose 1 life. Pay 2 life: deal 1 damage to any enemy creature or the enemy player. Use this only once each turn.
A splashy, dangerous relic: every upkeep it scorches every creature, including yours, and both players lose a life. Idle growers with counters and big duelists shrug it off while tokens and x/1s burn, and the once-per-turn ability turns extra life into extra damage. It cannot win alone, because it hurts its owner every turn too, which is the faith's lesson. A festival bonfire that will not go out is the party that has become a curse.

### Tribal cards (1.23)

The Revel's two tribes pull in the two directions the faith already pulls. Nobles are the slow half: lords who stay at home, grow fat on Idle counters and ping the enemy from their couches, so a Noble deck wants a wide table of loungers and a few walls, and its payoffs count the peerage (Toast to the Peerage, Elector of the Endless Banquet, Gossiping Marquis). Performers are the fast half: minstrels, tumblers, mummers, jesters and fire-eaters who arrive in a rush and hit at once, so a Performer deck curves out cheaply (Master of the Revels), gets haste and first strike from its leaders, and turns every new player in the troupe into damage. The two meet in the patron: the great house that keeps a company of players, which is exactly what Ruggiero rewards. Nobles are mostly existing cards (eight before this set, plus two duelists retagged as Noble Duelists); Performers needed the new cards and the Jester's retag to reach depth.

**Wandering Minstrel** · Common · Creature (Human Performer) · R · 1/1 · Haste (this can attack the turn it comes into play).
A one-drop that attacks at once, a point under the vanilla 2/1 to pay for haste. It is the cheapest Performer and the first card of the troupe curve, and it turns on Tumbler and Lord of Misrule early. The wandering minstrel on the road to a war he will only sing about is the Revel's light-hearted face.

**Tumbler of the Fair** · Common · Creature (Human Performer) · 1R · 1/2 · This gets +1/+0 for each other Performer you control.
The common kinship card: a poor 1/2 alone, a 3/2 or 4/2 in a real troupe. Kinship on power only keeps it an attacker, never a wall, which suits the Performers. Tumblers and acrobats were a fixture of every medieval fair, and the flavour keeps their danger comic.

**Mummer of the Twelfth Night** · Common · Creature (Human Performer) · 2R · 3/2 · First strike (this deals combat damage before creatures without first strike).
A 3/2 first striker for three, one toughness under a vanilla 3/3 to pay for the keyword. It is the troupe's sturdy common body. The English mummers' play, acted from house to house at Christmastide with a hero, a dragon and a wooden sword, is a real folk custom and the Revel's at its most innocent.

**Perfumed Lordling** · Common · Creature (Human Noble) · R · 0/2 · Idle (at the end of your turn, if this is untapped and didn't attack): if you control another Noble, deal 1 damage to the enemy player.
A one-mana Courtier on the Chaise for the Noble deck: since 1.27 his Idle needs another Noble beside him (the new tribe condition, which never counts the card itself). Outside the tribe the Courtier is the better card; inside it the Lordling is the first-turn play that starts the slow clock and counts toward every Noble payoff. The scented pomander held to the nose against the stink of the street is the young fop's whole armour.

**Count of the Velvet Glove** · Common · Creature (Human Noble Duelist) · 3R · 3/3 · First strike (this deals combat damage before creatures without first strike). Idle (at the end of your turn, if this is untapped and didn't attack): put a +1/+1 counter on this.
A 3/3 first striker for four that grows while it waits, the Noble answer to the Satyr. It bridges the faith's duelists and its idle nobles: it can defend well from day one and attack harder later. The count who accepts every challenge but always after the next feast is the faith's courtly cowardice played for a smile.

**Shouts of Encore** · Common · Spell (Revelry) · R · Performers you control get +2/+0 until end of turn.
The troupe's combat trick: one mana, power only, one turn, as the Revel's team bonuses must be. At instant speed it wins a block or adds several points after the blockers are chosen, but it does nothing without Performers. The crowd's roar for an encore is the moment a show becomes an assault.

**Toast to the Peerage** · Common · Spell (Revelry) · 2R · Deal 1 damage to the enemy player for each Noble you control.
The Noble finisher at common: three mana for as much face damage as you have lords, usually two or three. It never touches creatures, so it is reach, not removal, and it rewards the slow Noble board for having stayed alive. The round of toasts at a lord's table, each peer rising in turn, is the image.

**Master of the Revels** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Noble) · 2R · 2/3 · Your Performer creature cards cost 1 less to play (this reduces only generic mana).
The troupe's enabler and the bridge between the tribes: a Noble body who makes every Performer cheaper. It lets a Performer deck dump its hand by the fourth turn. The Master of the Revels was a real officer of the English court who hired and paid the players, and his account book is on the desk beside him.

**Master of the Pageant Wagon** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Performer) · 2R · 2/2 · Other Performers you control get +1/+0 and have haste (they can attack the turn they come into play).
The Performer lord: power and haste for the rest of the troupe, in the Revel's own colours. It does nothing for itself, a 2/2 for three, so it must be protected or answered. Pageant wagons carried the town players from street to street, and a company leaping down already performing is the haste made visible.

**Gossiping Marquis** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Noble) · 2R · 1/2 · This gets +1/+1 for each other Noble you control.
The Noble kinship card, on both power and toughness because Nobles are the faith's slow, durable half. With two other lords it is a 3/4 for three, which is a good blocker that also attacks. The courtier who knows every scandal and is the subject of most is the faith's vanity in a single line.

**Lord of Misrule** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Performer) · 1R · 1/2 · Whenever another Performer enters under your control, deal 1 damage to the enemy player.
The troupe's damage engine: every new player is a point of burn. It hits only the player, so it is a clock and never removal. The Lord of Misrule, a fool crowned for the Christmas feast while the true lord serves at table, is a real and good-humoured old custom of inversion.

**Royal Command Performance** · Uncommon · Spell (Revelry) · 2R · Deal 1 damage to any enemy creature or the enemy player for each Performer you control.
The Performer payoff spell: scaling burn that kills a big creature or ends the game when the troupe is large. With no Performers it does nothing, which keeps it honest. A command performance before the king, with all the fire thrown at once, is the troupe at its most dangerous.

**King of the Minstrels** · Rare · Creature (Human Performer) · 2RR · 3/3 · Haste (this can attack the turn it comes into play). Other Performers you control get +1/+1.
The rare lord: a hasty 3/3 that makes the whole troupe bigger in both numbers, so small Performers survive pings and trade up. It is a build-around because it changes every combat the troupe takes. Rex ministrallorum is a real medieval title: the King of the Minstrels held a court for the players of his district and collected their dues.

**Elector of the Endless Banquet** · Rare · Creature (Human Noble) · 3R · 2/4 · Idle (at the end of your turn, if this is untapped and didn't attack): put a +1/+1 counter on each Noble you control.
The Noble build-around: every turn he stays home, every lord at the table grows. It is slow and asks the Noble deck to stay back for several turns, which is the whole Idle bargain made tribal. The prince-electors of the Empire, wooed with dinners by every candidate, give the comic picture of a vote sold for a longer feast.

**Ruggiero, Patron of the Players** · Mythic · Creature (Human Noble) · 2RR · 2/5 · Performers you control have first strike (they deal combat damage before creatures without first strike). Idle (at the end of your turn, if this is untapped and didn't attack): deal 1 damage to any enemy creature or the enemy player for each Performer you control.
The mythic that joins the tribes: a Noble patron who never fights, whose players get first strike and whose idle evenings rain fire on the enemy. It is splashy with a full troupe but needs Performers to do anything and dies to 5 damage. The Renaissance prince who kept a company of players is a real figure of the courts, and his name comes from the knights of Italian romance.

### Third tribe (1.25)

The Satyrs are the Revel's small third tribe and the one that sits on its own hinge: half of them want to run and half want to stay at home. Goat-legged, horned, piping and drinking, they are classical revelry played for mischief rather than menace, and a Satyr deck asks the Idle question across the whole herd: the young ones charge with haste while the old ones lounge on the casks and grow the others or ping the enemy. The package is deliberately small, two cheap bodies, an Idle grower, one instant that counts the herd and one rare that joins both halves, plus the old Satyr of the Drowsy Vineyard and the Vine-Crowned Bacchant retagged, six members in all.

**Satyr Piper of the Pines** · Common · Creature (Satyr) · 1R · 1/2 · When this enters, Satyrs you control gain haste until end of turn (they can attack this turn).
Since 1.27 the piper calls the herd out at once with a temporary keyword: he and every Satyr played this turn may attack straight away. Alone it is a weak two-drop that hits for one; played after another Satyr it turns a slow turn into a charge. Haste is the Revel's, and it lasts one turn, which keeps it a tempo card no stronger than the old attack bonus. The piper leading the charge with a tune that means "charge" is the tribe's whole spirit.

**Wineskin Satyr** · Common · Creature (Satyr) · R · 1/1 · Idle (at the end of your turn, if this is untapped and didn't attack): rummage (draw a card, then discard a card).
The tribe's one-drop: a cheaper, smaller Loafer of the Arbour that counts as a Satyr for Panic in the Thicket and the Horned Lord. It filters the hand while it sits, and never gives card advantage, only card quality. The satyr drinking to the weather on a wet hillside is the faith's sloth at its most harmless.

**Old Satyr on the Wine-Cask** · Uncommon · Creature (Satyr) · 2R · 1/3 · Idle (at the end of your turn, if this is untapped and didn't attack): put a +1/+1 counter on target Satyr you control.
The tribe's slow engine: as long as he stays home, one Satyr a turn grows, and he may grow himself if nothing else is there. It is weaker than an anthem and needs a turn of patience each time, which keeps it fair at uncommon. The old satyr telling the young ones how it was in his day, without rising from his cask, is Silenus in all but name.

**Panic in the Thicket** · Uncommon · Spell (Revelry) · 1R · Deal 1 damage to target enemy creature for each Satyr you control.
The Satyr payoff spell and the faith's one new instant here: creature-only burn that scales with the herd, so it is a cheap trick with two Satyrs and real removal with four. It never touches the player, which keeps it removal and not a finisher. "Panic" comes from the old shepherds' tale of the god of the wild whose shout scattered armies; here the noise is only pipes and hooves in the dark, and it is the sentries who run.

**Horned Lord of the Rout** · Rare · Creature (Satyr) · 2RR · 2/4 · Other Satyrs you control have haste (they can attack the turn they come into play). Idle (at the end of your turn, if this is untapped and didn't attack): deal 1 damage to the enemy player for each Satyr you control.
The build-around that joins the two halves of the tribe: he gives the herd haste so it can charge at once, and rewards himself for staying behind with damage for every Satyr. With three Satyrs he is three points a turn, but he hits only the player and dies to 4 damage, so he is a clock to be answered, never a lock. The lord who sends the rout down the hill and follows at his own pace, which is to say not at all, is the Revel's comedy of excess in a single figure.
