## The Technocracy (C)

The Technocracy plays like an engine you assemble piece by piece: cheap Constructs and Servo tokens widen the board, Assembly creatures grow with every machine beside them, and cost reducers and Overclock turn spare mana into tempo. Its answers are Snare relics that lock enemy creatures in place rather than destroy them, and its biggest threats are cheap machines with an upkeep you must keep paying. Cards that cost only generic mana are deliberately modest so they cannot shore up another faith's weaknesses, while cards with the C symbol run at full rate for decks built on Foundries.

**Brass Scuttler** · Common · Creature (Construct) · 1 · 1/1 · Sacrifice this: put a +1/+1 counter on target Construct you control.

A one-drop that is never quite dead weight: early it chips in, late it melts itself down to make a bigger Construct bigger. At one generic mana it is a stat point under rate, and its ability does nothing for a deck without Constructs, so it gives other faiths very little. Spare parts that walk are the Technocracy's idea of thrift.

**Cog Drone** · Common · Creature (Construct) · C · 1/1 · Assembly (this gets +1/+1 for each other Construct you control).

The cheapest Assembly creature and the first sign that the faith wants a crowded board. A 1/1 for one is weak on turn one but scales with every Construct after it, and the C symbol keeps it out of other decks. The flavour states the faith's arithmetic plainly: the part is worth nothing, the whole is worth everything.

**Tinkerer's Apprentice** · Common · Creature (Human Artificer) · 1C · 1/1 · When this enters, create a 1/1 Servo Construct token.

Two bodies for two mana, which feeds Assembly, Servo sacrifice and snare defence all at once. In set 1 it was colourless and fit any deck; the C symbol now keeps that value for the faith that uses Servos best. The apprentice artificer is the human face of the guild's workshops.

**Clockwork Hound** · Common · Creature (Construct Hound) · 1C · 2/2 · Overclock 2 (you may pay 2 more as you play this). If overclocked, it enters with two +1/+1 counters.

The faith's clean introduction to Overclock: a 2/2 on turn two or a 4/4 on turn four, both at full rate because of the C symbol. A flexible common like this smooths the curve without adding complexity. The flavour line about honest loyalty is kept from set 1 because it sums up the guild's transactional ethic.

**Steam Hauler** · Common · Creature (Construct) · 1C · 3/3 · Upkeep 1 (at the beginning of your upkeep, pay 1 or sacrifice this).

The common face of the upkeep mechanic: a 3/3 for two that costs a mana every turn. Early it trades far above its cost, but later it taxes your development, which is the point. It costs C so that other faiths cannot borrow a cheap fatty, and its coal bill is the guild's ledger made flesh, or rather brass.

**Rivetwall Sentry** · Common · Creature (Construct) · 1C · 0/3 · Assembly (this gets +1/+1 for each other Construct you control).

A wall that becomes an attacker. Alone it is a 0/3 blocker for two; with two other Constructs it is a 2/5. It replaces the set 1 defender version, since defender belongs to the Stoa, and gives the faith early defence that rewards building out. The doorway flavour is kept from set 1.

**Piston Laborer** · Common · Creature (Construct) · 2C · 3/3 · (No abilities.)

A vanilla 3/3 for three at full rate, the reliable body every faith needs at common. The C symbol is what earns full stats. Its tireless work is the faith's efficiency over passion in one line.

**Scrap Collector** · Common · Creature (Human Artificer) · 2C · 2/3 · Sacrifice a Servo: put a +1/+1 counter on this.

The common payoff for spare Servos: it eats them to grow, turning a board of chump blockers into one real threat. A 2/3 for three is a point under rate to pay for the ability. The scavenging artificer shows the guild's habit of wasting nothing, not even its own.

**Gearwright Journeyman** · Common · Creature (Human Artificer) · 3 · 2/3 · Overclock 2 (you may pay 2 more as you play this). If overclocked, when this enters, create two 1/1 Servo Construct tokens.

An Overclock card whose extra payment buys bodies instead of counters. As a generic card it is modest: a 2/3 for three, or three small bodies for five. Other faiths can play it, but Servos give them nothing they lack, while a Technocracy deck turns the tokens into Assembly and sacrifice fuel.

**Bronze Phalanx Automaton** · Common · Creature (Construct) · 3C · 3/4 · (No abilities.)

A plain 3/4 for four, a point under rate as the faith's sturdy filler, with vigilance removed because vigilance belongs to the Stoa. It holds the ground while the engine assembles. The flavour keeps its pointed contrast between virtue and mechanism.

**Foundry Golem** · Common · Creature (Construct) · 4 · 3/4 · Overclock 2 (you may pay 2 more as you play this). If overclocked, it enters with two +1/+1 counters.

A generic Overclock body: a 3/4 for four or a 5/6 for six, both a point under rate as befits a card any deck can play. It gives the faith a late game sink at common. 'Animated by arithmetic' is the Technocracy in three words.

**Smelter Automaton** · Common · Creature (Construct) · 4C · 4/3 · When this enters, create a 1/1 Servo Construct token.

A five-drop that brings a helper: a 4/3 plus a 1/1, a point of toughness under full rate in exchange for the second body. Two Constructs in one card is exactly what Assembly wants. The side vent that spits out a Servo is the comic, slightly sinister edge of the foundry.

**Cogspire Hulk** · Common · Creature (Construct) · 6 · 5/6 · (No abilities.)

The common top end: a generic 5/6 for six, a stat point under rate. Any deck can run it, but a big vanilla body takes nothing away from another faith's identity. The invoice joke is the guild's accounts in miniature.

**Spare Parts** · Common · Spell (Device) · 1 · Create a 1/1 Servo Construct token. Overclock 2 (you may pay 2 more as you play this). If overclocked, create another Servo.

A cheap token spell that scales: one Servo for one mana, two for three. It is generic, so it is priced softly; a 1/1 for one is a point under rate. It turns spare mana into Assembly counts and sacrifice fodder.

**Foundry Requisition** · Common · Spell (Order) · 1C · Create two 1/1 Servo Construct tokens.

Two bodies for two mana at full rate, the committed version of Spare Parts. It is the plainest way to widen the board for Assembly. The requisition slip, stamped and billed, is how the Technocracy wages war.

**Tempered Plating** · Common · Spell (Device) · 2C · Put two +1/+1 counters on target Construct you control.

A permanent upgrade instead of a temporary pump, so it stays clear of the Old Ways' combat tricks. In 1.23 it became an instant and its cost rose by one generic: two permanent counters in the middle of combat are worth three mana, not two. The Construct restriction keeps it inside the faith, and the guild stamp makes even armour a matter of paperwork.

**Calibrated Discharge** · Common · Spell (Device) · 1 · Put a +1/+1 counter on target Construct you control. Overclock 1C (you may pay 1C more as you play this). If overclocked, also deal 2 damage to target enemy creature.

The faith's common creature damage, kept behind an Overclock that needs a Foundry, so other faiths cannot treat it as cheap removal. Base mode is a small permanent upgrade for one; overclocked it is three mana for a counter and two damage, below the Revel's rate. In 1.23 it became an instant at the same cost, since a single counter, or two damage behind a Foundry overclock, is modest enough to answer an attack without a surcharge. The precise flavour keeps the cold, measured tone of set 1.

**Gear Trap** · Common · Relic (Snare) · 1C · When this enters, choose target enemy creature with power 3 or less. It can't attack or block while this stays in play.

The cheap snare, limited to small and medium creatures. Two mana to shut down an early threat is the faith's answer to aggression, and it costs C so it stays Technocracy removal. A trap built by clerks fits a faith that wins by procedure.

**Brass Manacles** · Common · Relic (Snare) · 2C · When this enters, choose target enemy creature. It can't attack or block while this stays in play.

The unconditional common snare, priced at three like a classic Pacifism with the blocking clause added. It answers a creature without destroying it, which keeps the faith clear of destroy effects and leaves room for relic removal to free the prisoner. The courts of Cogspire make even justice mechanical.

**Calibration Bench** · Common · Relic (Workshop) · 2 · Tap, pay 1C: put a +1/+1 counter on target Construct you control.

A slow, repeatable mana sink that rewards long games with a board of Constructs. The relic is cheap, but the activation needs a Foundry, so the bench does nothing in another faith's deck. Steady improvement by measurement is the faith's notion of progress.

**Siege Automaton** · Uncommon · Creature (Construct) · 2C · 5/5 · Upkeep 2 (at the beginning of your upkeep, pay 2 or sacrifice this).

A 5/5 for three is two full points above rate, paid for with two mana every turn. It is the uncommon step up from Steam Hauler, a real threat on turn three that slows your later turns. Trample is gone because it belongs to the Old Ways; the patient flavour is kept.

**Cogspire Foreman** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Artificer) · 1C · 1/2 · Tap, pay 2: create a 1/1 Servo Construct token.

A slow token engine on a fragile body: three mana a turn for a 1/1 is fair, and it makes every later Servo payoff better. The tap cost means it must survive a turn first. A foreman who points while others build is the guild's hierarchy in one man.

**Linked Automaton** · Uncommon · Creature (Construct) · 2C · 1/1 · Assembly (this gets +1/+1 for each other Construct you control). Overclock 2 (you may pay 2 more as you play this). If overclocked, when this enters, create two 1/1 Servo Construct tokens.

Overclock and Assembly in one card: overclocked for five, it brings two Servos and is at least a 3/3 itself. Unkicked it is a small Assembly body for three. It shows how the faith's mechanics feed one another, which is the uncommon twist.

**Furnace Stoker** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Artificer) · 1C · 2/1 · Sacrifice a Servo: put a +1/+1 counter on target Construct you control.

The flexible Servo outlet: unlike Scrap Collector, it moves the counters to whichever Construct needs them. A 2/1 for two is at rate with a useful ability. The stoker who feeds small machines to big ones shows the faith's cold sums.

**Trip-Hammer Automaton** · Uncommon · Creature (Construct) · 3C · 2/4 · Whenever this attacks, put a +1/+1 counter on it.

A machine that warms up: a 2/4 for four that grows every time it swings. It is under rate at first and well above it after two attacks, so it rewards keeping it alive. The steam hammer gaining force with each stroke is the faith's relentless efficiency.

**Gearwork Phalanx** · Uncommon · Creature (Construct) · 4 · 2/3 · Assembly (this gets +1/+1 for each other Construct you control).

A generic Assembly creature, priced well under rate because alone it is a 2/3 for four. In a deck full of Constructs it becomes the largest body on the board. Other faiths can play it, but without Constructs it stays small, which is exactly how colourless cards should behave.

**Mass Retooling** · Uncommon · Spell (Order) · 2C · Put a +1/+1 counter on each creature you control. Overclock 2 (you may pay 2 more as you play this). If overclocked, put another +1/+1 counter on each creature you control.

A one-time upgrade for a wide board, permanent counters rather than a static anthem, which the Church owns. It is strongest after a turn of Servos, and Overclock doubles it for five mana. A guild order that retools the whole line in a morning shows efficiency at scale.

**Expedited Patent** · Uncommon · Spell (Order) · 1C · Create a 1/1 Servo Construct token, then put two +1/+1 counters on target Construct you control.

The C symbol makes this a card for committed decks. It adds a body and two counters for two mana, four points of stats at rate, and the counters can land on the fresh Servo or on a better Construct. The fee that buys speed is the guild's commerce in a sentence.

**Bulk Order** · Uncommon · Spell (Order) · 3C · Create three 1/1 Servo Construct tokens.

Three bodies for four mana, a wide board in one card. It sets up Assembly, Mass Retooling and the sacrifice outlets, and it rebuilds after a sweeper. The flavour turns economies of scale into something quietly chilling.

**Galvanic Coil** · Uncommon · Relic (Engine) · 2C · At the beginning of your upkeep, deal 1 damage to target enemy creature.

Set 1's Coil fit any deck and could hit players; now it costs C and hits only creatures, as the spec requires. One damage a turn picks off X/1s and finishes creatures hurt in combat. It is one of the faith's very few damage sources, and the crackle at dawn keeps its set 1 flavour.

**Self-Mending Forge** · Uncommon · Relic (Workshop) · 3C · At the beginning of your upkeep, create a 1/1 Servo Construct token.

A steady stream of Servos for four mana, and the core of many Technocracy engines. It now costs C, so other faiths cannot borrow a free token stream. The forge that needs no smith is the faith's dream of labour without men.

**Foundry Ledger** · Uncommon · Relic (Engine) · 3 · Your Technocracy cards cost 1 less to play. This reduces only generic mana.

The plain cost reducer that replaces the old mana rocks: it speeds up only Technocracy cards, so it does nothing in a Church or Revel deck. It is priced at three so that a deck must invest before it pays off. The ledger as a source of power is the guild's faith in accounts.

**Clockwork Gaol** · Uncommon · Relic (Snare) · C · When this enters, choose target enemy creature. It can't attack or block while this stays in play. Upkeep 1 (at the beginning of your upkeep, pay 1 or sacrifice this).

A one-mana snare that you must keep paying for, combining two of the faith's mechanics. It can shut down a dangerous creature of your choice on turn one, but every mana spent on it is a mana not spent building. When the payments stop, the prisoner walks free.

**Guild Comptroller** · Rare · Creature (Human Clerk) · 1C · 1/3 · Your Technocracy cards cost 1 less to play. This reduces only generic mana.

A cost reducer on a body, the faith's answer to mana creatures without giving any other faith ramp. On turn two it makes a three-drop cost two, and it stacks with Foundry Ledger for a fast engine. A clerk as the most powerful man in the room is the Technocracy's whole character.

**Grand Foundry Colossus** · Rare · Creature (Construct) · 3C · 6/6 · Upkeep 3.

A 6/6 for four is enormous, and three mana every upkeep is a heavy price that can starve the rest of your turn. It is the build-around for decks with reducers and cheap Constructs, since those leave mana free to feed it. The colossus that eats coal seams shows the faith's scale and its cost.

**Cogspire Assembler** · Rare · Creature (Construct) · 4C · 2/2 · Assembly. At the beginning of your upkeep, create a 1/1 Servo Construct token.

A self-feeding engine: every upkeep it makes a Servo, and every Servo adds to its Assembly bonus. A 2/2 for five is weak at first, so it must survive a turn to matter, which keeps it answerable. It is the purest picture of the faith's loop of making and growing.

**Arc-Welder Construct** · Rare · Creature (Construct) · 3C · 3/3 · Overclock 2. If overclocked, when this enters, deal 3 damage to target enemy creature.

Overclock used for a stronger effect rather than counters: six mana for a 3/3 and three damage to a creature. Set 1's version could hit players and fit any deck; now it costs C and targets only creatures. It is one of the faith's three damage cards, kept at rare so it stays scarce.

**Mainspring Juggernaut** · Rare · Creature (Construct) · 2C · 2/2 · Upkeep 1. At the beginning of your upkeep, put a +1/+1 counter on this.

A clock that must be wound: each upkeep you pay one and it grows. It starts as a 2/2 for three and becomes a 5/5 by the end of its third turn, if you keep paying. The decision to keep winding or let it stop is the upkeep mechanic at its most personal.

**Boilerhouse Overseer** · Rare · Creature (Human Artificer) · 2C · 2/3 · Sacrifice a Servo: put a +1/+1 counter on each other creature you control. Activate only once each turn.

The rare Servo payoff: each turn one Servo becomes a counter on every other creature. Limited to once a turn so it cannot snowball in a single main phase. It rewards a steady Servo supply from Cogspire Foreman or Self-Mending Forge, and the furnace image shows sacrifice as sheer arithmetic.

**Cogspire Exhibition** · Rare · Spell (Order) · 3CC · Create two 1/1 Servo Construct tokens, then put a +1/+1 counter on each creature you control.

The faith's rare finisher: on a board of a few Constructs it adds two bodies and grows everything by one, Servos included, for good. Two C symbols keep it for committed decks. It is a single big turn, not a lasting anthem, and the exhibition hall of gleaming machines is the Technocracy showing off.

**Cogspire Lockworks** · Rare · Relic (Snare) · 2CC · When this enters, choose two target enemy creatures. They can't attack or block while this stays in play.

A two-for-one snare at four mana, the rare version of Brass Manacles. It can stop the two enemy creatures you most fear, but a single relic answer frees both, which keeps it fair. The clerk who loses the key on purpose is the faith's bureaucratic menace.

**Perpetual Engine** · Rare · Relic (Engine) · 2 · Your Technocracy cards cost 2 less to play. This reduces only generic mana. Upkeep 1.

Set 1's Perpetual Engine was ramp for any deck; now it reduces only Technocracy costs, so it is useless elsewhere. Two off every Technocracy card is powerful, so it charges one mana each upkeep, which pays for itself only when you play two or more cards a turn. The name is ironic: nothing in the guild runs for free.

**The Prime Engine** · Mythic · Creature (Construct) · 5CC · 4/4 · Assembly. When this enters, create two 1/1 Servo Construct tokens.

The faith's showpiece: seven mana for a 4/4 that arrives with two Servos and is at least a 6/6 at once. It is a single creature at the centre of the board, so removal on it still leaves the opponent facing only 1/1s. Vigilance and trample are gone as off-faith keywords, and the 'echo' flavour is kept from set 1.

**The Iron Leviathan** · Mythic · Creature (Construct) · 3CC · 6/6 · Assembly. Upkeep 2.

Assembly and upkeep together on the biggest machine in the faith: a 6/6 for five that grows with every Construct and costs two mana each turn. It is splashy and fearsome, but the upkeep and a single answer keep it from winning alone. The war machine still on the books is the Technocracy's cold accounting at its grandest.

**The Grand Tabulator** · Mythic · Relic (Engine) · 4C · Your Technocracy cards cost 1 less to play. This reduces only generic mana. At the beginning of your upkeep, create a 1/1 Servo Construct token.

The guild's great calculating engine combines the faith's two engine pieces, cost reduction and a Servo every turn. Five mana is a heavy investment for a relic that does nothing to the board on the turn it arrives. It is the soulless efficiency of the Technocracy given a throne, and its final sum is the faith's quiet horror.

### Tribal cards (1.23)

The Technocracy's two tribes are the made and the makers. Constructs are the wide tribe: Servos, Assembly bodies and upkeep giants already filled the faith, so the new Construct cards mostly reward building alongside the guild's craftsmen. Artificers are the narrow tribe, and they get their own job: they cut the cost of Constructs, grow whenever a Construct enters, and spend Overclock mana on effects that scale with how many craftsmen sit at the bench. A Technocracy deck now chooses between a flood of cheap machines and a smaller workshop of Artificers who make every machine count, and the best decks run a little of both, since each tribe feeds the other.

**Bellows Boy** · Common · Creature (Human Artificer) · C · 1/1 · Whenever a Construct enters under your control, this gets +1/+0 until end of turn.

The cheapest Artificer and the plainest statement of the tribe's job: it watches the machines arrive and is livelier for it. On a turn with a Servo spell it attacks as a 2/1 or 3/1, but it is a frail one-drop that dies to any blocker, so it rewards timing rather than raw stats. The boy at the bellows was the first rung of every guild apprenticeship.

**Journeyman Riveter** · Common · Creature (Human Artificer) · 1C · 1/1 · When this enters, put a +1/+1 counter on target Construct you control.

Three points of stats for two mana, split between a small body and a permanent counter, at the C rate. It does nothing on an empty board, which keeps it inside a Construct deck. Riveting was the journeyman's daily work in every shipyard and foundry: unglamorous, exact and endlessly repeated.

**Workshop Draughtsman** · Common · Creature (Human Artificer) · 2C · 2/3 · Overclock 2 (you may pay 2 more as you play this). If overclocked, when this enters, put a +1/+1 counter on each Artificer you control.

The common Overclock payoff for Artificers: a plain 2/3 for three, or for five a 3/4 that lifts every other craftsman too. It counts itself, so it is never a blank, and it grows better the more of the tribe you field. The master's drawing passed round the workshop is how medieval masons and clockmakers spread their method.

**Tended Automaton** · Common · Creature (Construct) · 1C · 2/2 · This gets +1/+0 for each Artificer you control.

A Construct that wants its makers nearby, the mirror of Assembly: instead of counting machines it counts men. Alone it is a fair 2/2 for two; with two Artificers it hits as a 4/2 while staying easy to block and kill. Power only keeps it modest at common. The boy who names the machine he polishes is the faith's one touch of warmth, and it is a little sad.

**Coupling Automaton** · Common · Creature (Construct) · 2C · 2/2 · Whenever another Construct enters under your control, put a +1/+1 counter on this.

A permanent version of Assembly: it starts under rate as a 2/2 for three but keeps every counter it earns, even if the Servos later die or are sacrificed. That makes it a good partner for Scrap Collector and Furnace Stoker, which consume the tokens it has already counted. The chain of coupled machines is the Cogspire line in miniature.

**Emergency Shoring** · Common · Spell (Order) · 2C · Put a +1/+1 counter on each Construct you control.

A team-wide combat trick for machines, priced a mana above sorcery-speed effects as instants are, and limited to Constructs so it never becomes an anthem for other creatures. Cast during the enemy's attack it turns a row of Servos into 2/2 blockers for good. A guild order issued mid-battle, with the bill to follow, is the Technocracy at war.

**Mule-Frame Automaton** · Common · Creature (Construct) · 3C · 3/3 · When this enters, put a +1/+1 counter on each Artificer you control.

A four-mana 3/3 is a point under rate; the counters make up the difference only when you have Artificers on the table. It is the Construct side of the bargain: machines that carry the craftsmen's burdens and leave them stronger. Pack frames for tools and coal were the first automata a medieval guild would have dreamed of.

**Guild Fitter** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Artificer) · 1C · 1/2 · Your Construct creature cards cost 1 less to play. This reduces only generic mana.

The tribal cost reducer: narrower than Guild Comptroller, since it helps only Construct creatures, and so cheaper and at uncommon. It turns three-drop Constructs into two-drops and stacks with the faith's Engines for very fast starts. A fitter with parts already cut is the medieval workshop's answer to mass production.

**Foundry Line-Master** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Artificer) · 3C · 2/3 · Constructs you control get +1/+1.

The Construct lord, held by an Artificer so that the anthem dies with the man, not the machines. Servos become 2/2s and Assembly bodies grow further, which makes him the first target of enemy removal. Team-wide anthems belong to the Church, so this one is filtered to the faith's own tribe, as the spec allows. The overseer with lamp and gauge stands for guild quality control.

**Arc-Lamp Galvanist** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Artificer) · 2C · 2/2 · Overclock 1C (you may pay 1C more as you play this). If overclocked, when this enters, deal 1 damage to target enemy creature for each Artificer you control.

One of the faith's rare damage cards, kept behind an Overclock that needs a Foundry and scaling with the tribe, so other faiths gain nothing from it. With two other Artificers out it is five mana for a 2/2 and three damage, in line with Arc-Welder Construct. The galvanist is natural philosophy turned into an invoice.

**Field Repair** · Uncommon · Spell (Device) · 1C · Put a +1/+1 counter on target Construct you control for each Artificer you control.

The Artificers' combat trick: the more craftsmen on the field, the bigger the permanent repair. With one Artificer it is a weak two-mana instant; with three it saves a Construct in combat and keeps the counters afterward. It is permanent, so it stays clear of the Old Ways' temporary pumps.

**Masterwork Automaton** · Uncommon · Creature (Construct) · 3C · 2/3 · This gets +1/+1 for each Artificer you control.

The uncommon Construct that rewards an Artificer deck: alone a soft 2/3 for four, but with two craftsmen a 4/5. It gives the tribes a shared finisher at uncommon. Guild masterpieces were judged by the masters of the craft, and the punch marks stand for each master's approval.

**Master Horologist** · Rare · Creature (Human Artificer) · 2C · 2/2 · Other Artificers you control get +1/+1. Your Construct creature cards cost 1 less to play. This reduces only generic mana.

The Artificer lord and the bridge between the tribes: he makes his fellow craftsmen sturdier and his machines cheaper. A 2/2 for three is fragile, so he must be protected to keep the engine running. The horologist who set the city clock recalls the great medieval astronomical clocks of Strasbourg and Wells, the proudest work of the age's craftsmen.

**Self-Winding Wright** · Rare · Creature (Construct Artificer) · 3C · 2/2 · Assembly (this gets +1/+1 for each other Construct you control). Whenever another Artificer enters under your control, create a 1/1 Servo Construct token.

The only card in both tribes: a machine that builds machines, which counts for every Artificer and Construct payoff. Each new craftsman brings a Servo, and each Servo grows the Wright through Assembly, so it is a build-around that rewards a deck balanced between the tribes. A 2/2 for four is weak on its own, which keeps it answerable. The joke about wages is the guild's dream and the faith's quiet horror.

**The Archwright of Cogspire** · Mythic · Creature (Human Artificer) · 2CC · 2/4 · Whenever another Construct enters under your control, put a +1/+1 counter on each Artificer you control. Overclock 3 (you may pay 3 more as you play this). If overclocked, when this enters, create three 1/1 Servo Construct tokens.

The faith's tribal showpiece: every machine that enters makes every craftsman stronger, and overclocked he arrives with three Servos, putting three counters on himself and each other Artificer at once. It is splashy but answerable: the counters stay, but without him the engine stops, and he is a single creature with no protection. The master whose hand or pupils drew every machine is the guild's highest rank, and its proudest boast.

### Third tribe (1.25)

The Technocracy's third theme is not a creature tribe but its own gear: relics and the new Equipment. The tribe word "Relic" counts every relic you control, so the faith's existing Snares, Workshops and Engines (Gear Trap, Brass Manacles, Calibration Bench, Galvanic Coil, Self-Mending Forge, Foundry Ledger, Clockwork Gaol, Cogspire Lockworks, Perpetual Engine, The Grand Tabulator) already belong to it, together with the two new Equipment: twelve relics in all, and no retagging was needed. The five new cards are two cheap colourless Equipment that feed the count early and three payoffs that count relics or reward each one that enters (a kinship common, a cost cutter with an ally trigger, and a rare engine). Equipment lend first strike and vigilance, which the spec allows on Technocracy Equipment only; the faith still owns no keyword on its creatures. There is no energy or other new resource: everything runs on mana, relics in play and the cards already in the spec.

**Spring-Loaded Lance** · Common · Relic (Equipment) · 1 · Equipped creature gets +1/+0 and has first strike. Equip 2 (pay 2: attach this to a creature you control. Equip only as a sorcery.)

The cheapest relic in the faith, so it turns on the relic payoffs from the first turns and can be played with no creature out. +1/+0 and first strike is a modest bonus at early-2000s rates (Leonin Scimitar gave +1/+1 for 1, Equip 1), paid for with the dearer Equip 2. First strike stays a Revel keyword on creatures; here it is only lent by a machine, which the spec allows for Equipment. A lance that fires its point forward on a spring is the guild's answer to the joust.

**Tool-Rack Automaton** · Common · Creature (Construct) · 1C · 1/3 · This gets +1/+0 for each relic you control.

The common relic payoff, shaped like Tended Automaton: a cheap Construct that is a wall on its own and an attacker once the bench is full. With two relics out it is a 3/3 for two, and it counts for Assembly as well, so it fits either Technocracy deck. A machine that carries every tool of the workshop on its back is the faith's idea of a good labourer.

**Riveted Pauldron** · Uncommon · Relic (Equipment) · 3 · When this enters, attach it to target creature you control. Equipped creature gets +1/+2 and has vigilance. Equip 2 (pay 2: attach this to a creature you control. Equip only as a sorcery.)

The uncommon twist is that it straps itself on as it enters, so it works the turn it is played; afterward it moves for Equip 2 like any Equipment. +1/+2 and vigilance makes any body an attacker that still guards, which suits the faith's slow, sturdy machines. Three mana is a point dear for the bonus, the price of the free first attachment. Vigilance is a Stoa keyword, lent here only through Equipment as the spec allows.

**Guild Armourer** · Uncommon · Creature (Human Artificer) · 2C · 2/2 · Your relic cards cost 1 less to play. This reduces only generic mana. Whenever a relic enters under your control, put a +1/+1 counter on this.

The relic cost cutter, at the same rate as Guild Fitter but for relics, and an Artificer, so he also counts for that tribe's payoffs. Each relic that enters makes him bigger for good, so in a relic deck he grows into a 4/4 or more while cheapening the Equipment that make him grow. Equip costs are not reduced, which keeps him honest. The armourer who measures rather than trusts is the Technocracy's craftsman at his most typical.

**Arsenal Automaton** · Rare · Creature (Construct) · 3C · 2/2 · This gets +1/+1 for each relic you control. Whenever a relic enters under your control, create a 1/1 Servo Construct token.

The build-around: every relic both grows it and brings a Servo, so cheap Equipment become two-for-one cards and the Servos feed Assembly and the Construct lords. A 2/2 for four with no relics is weak, and Old Ways relic removal or any creature removal stops it, so it is splashy but answerable. The guild filing a frame hung with spare gear as a garrison is the faith's quiet joke about soldiers who need no wages.
